# AcuPulse regulatory language and do's and don'ts

This page covers the **Aesthetics** AcuPulse CO2 laser family. Every source on file is an Aesthetics document; no Eye Care document exists for this product, so there is no Eye Care section. It records what the newest Lumenis labeling and marketing sources say. It is not an approval, and every piece still goes through legal and regulatory review.

No Lumenis legal do's-and-don'ts word list for AcuPulse was found among the sources. The do's and don'ts below are derived only from the quoted labeling and disclaimer text; they are not a legal word list.

Quotes are verbatim from the extracted PDF text. Line breaks are joined, and spelling is kept as printed in the source (for example "thorasic"). Where the source states a market, it is shown. Where a quote comes from the PDF text of an FDA letter, obvious OCR digit-for-letter errors are corrected (C02 to CO2, 4OWG to 40WG) and said so.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

### General and dermatology/plastic surgery (aesthetic) contraindications

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.3 "General Contraindications" (pp.12-7 to 12-8):**
>
> - Do not use the CO2 laser on hard tissues, such as bone or teeth.
> - Do not use the CO2 laser for cutting or ablating dense, healthy bone or bone marrow (for example, hard palate and mandible).
> - Do not use the CO2 laser on vessels greater than 0.5 mm in diameter, as hemostasis may not be effective
> - Do not use the CO2 laser where a clinical procedure is precluded by anesthesia requirements, site access, or other general operative considerations.
> - Do not use the CO2 laser if the following conditions apply to the patient: Any concurrent cancer or history of skin cancer, except treatment of specifically indicated skin cancer (see Dermatology indications for use, below). Any concurrent skin condition such as active infections. Immune compromise. Is receiving anticoagulation treatment or has a thromboembolic condition. Pregnancy. Is using of photosensitive medication for which light exposure is contraindicated.
> - Do not use the CO2 laser if the following conditions apply to the treated area: Recent exposure to sun or artificial tanning, presence of a deep suntan, sunburn or artificially toned skin. Infection. Active Herpes simplex (treatment can be given post preventive treatment).

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.6.2 "Dermatology and Plastic Surgery Contraindications" (p.12-11):** Patients should not be considered for laser skin resurfacing procedures if they: Have taken Accutane® (Isotretinoin) within the past 6-12 months. Have a history of keloid formation. Have a history of poor wound healing. Demonstrate excessive or unusually prolonged erythema or edema (post- appropriate laser test patching) that may lead to hyper-pigmentation, or hypopigmentation.

The AcuPulse W manual (UM-0000777USB Rev C, pp.12-1 to 12-2) carries the same two lists, read side by side with the above. The AcuPulse manual's Ch. 11.3 (p.11-2) adds contraindications for surgical (non-aesthetic) applications, including "Do not use the CO2 laser on vessels greater than 0.5 mm in diameter, as hemostasis may not be effective". AcuPulse W Ch. 11 adds ENT, neurosurgery and general and thoracic contraindications that are outside aesthetic copy and are not reproduced here.

Have active vaginal or vulvar infection i.e., (herpes, candida, STDs). Pregnant or within 3 months postpartum. Have prolapse beyond hymen. Have history of radiation to the pelvic region. Have history of reconstructive pelvic surgery with "mesh kits". Have a history of impaired wound healing. Have a history of keloid formation. Known anticoagulation treatment or thromboembolic condition. *Non-exhaustive list

## Warnings, cautions and expected effects (verbatim)

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.5 (p.12-9):** Warning: There is a risk of infection and scarring associated with any CO2 procedure. Therefore, appropriate pre- and post-treatment care should always be practiced. Caution: Laser test patching in a small inconspicuous area within the treatment zone is recommended prior to treatment to validate desired parameters and to avoid unexpected results.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.4 (p.12-8):** The physician must select appropriate patients and manage the pre- and post-operative care. [...] As with conventional non-laser procedures, there is no guarantee that treatment with the CO2 laser will entirely eliminate any lesion entity. Repeat treatment or alternative therapies subsequently may be required.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.6.3 (p.12-11):** Caution: In some treatments (for example tattoo removal), scar tissue formation has been reported which may fade or become permanent. Therefore, use of the CO2 laser for cosmetic removal of dermal skin lesions should be performed with caution since there is a potential for scar formation.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.6.4 (pp.12-11 to 12-12):** General dermatology and plastic surgery potential complications include: Scarring; Ulceration; Persistent edema and/or erythema; Infection. General dermatology and plastic surgery expected side effects include: Erythema that resolves over time; Swelling/edema that resolves over time. [...] Laser skin resurfacing potential complications include: Hypopigmentation; Hyperpigmentation that resolves over time; Scarring that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment; Induration that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment; Formation of fibrotic tissue that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment; Preauricular flap necrosis; Postauricular skin slough/loss. Laser skin resurfacing possible short-term reactions include: Transient pain that is observed immediately post-operatively and generally resolves quickly; Transient burning sensation that is observed immediately post-operatively and generally resolves quickly; Itching that generally resolves within the first 2 weeks post-operatively; Sensation of tightness that resolves over time. Laser skin resurfacing possible medium-term reactions include: Crusting that is observed immediately post-operatively through 2 weeks postoperatively; Formation of Milia that resolves over time; Contact irritant dermatitis to postoperative topical agents (primarily antibiotics); Reactivation of herpes simplex.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.6.4 (p.12-13), caution on skin type:** The incidence and duration of post-operative hyperpigmentation may be higher in patients with darker skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-VI,) than in patients with lighter skin types (Fitzpatrick I-III). Selection of treatment parameters should be considered carefully to reduce the post-inflammatory response and care should be employed to perform test patching before the procedure within sufficient period of time as per skin type.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.7 (p.12-13):** When using Combo mode, as compared to Superficial or Deep alone, consider prolonged treatment downtime.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 12.7 (p.12-13):** Therefore, the accumulated effect of treatment is expected over time and should be explained to the patient.

> **UM-1091300USBe Rev A, Ch. 7.2 (p.7-2) and UM-1044640Be Rev C (p.4):** Lasers generate a highly-concentrated beam of light which may cause injury if improperly used. To protect the patient and operating personnel, users should read and comprehend all information provided in this operator's manual and in the operator manuals provided with Lumenis CO2 laser systems. [...] Laser surgical procedures should be performed only by individuals adequately trained in laser surgery and clinical use of CO2 lasers. [...] All personnel in the treatment room, including patients, must wear adequate protective eyewear when the CO2 laser is in use.

(The Ch. 7.2 text says "adequate protective eyewear"; the AcuScan120 IFU says "protective eyewear". The first sentence is common to both.)

The AcuPulse W manual (UM-0000777USB Rev C, pp.12-2 to 12-5) carries the same aesthetic cautions, complications and Fitzpatrick IV-VI caution.

### Standard "Warnings and Risks" block on marketing pieces

> **PB-00082580 Rev A (2025), p.7 (B2B brochure):** CO2 lasers are intended solely for use by physicians trained in the use of the Carbon Dioxide laser (10.6 μm) wavelength. Incorrect treatment settings or misuse of the technology can present risk of serious injury to patient and operating personnel. Risks that may be associated with any CO2 laser procedure may include change of pigmentation, infection, erythema, skin induration or scarring. Read and understand the CO2 systems and accessories operator manuals for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks. The use of Lumenis® CO2 laser is contraindicated where a patient has taken Accutane (Isotretinoin) within the past 6-12 months, has a history of keloid formation and demonstrate excessive or unusually prolonged erythema.

> **PB-00077240 Rev A (2024), p.1 (patient brochure):** the same block, headed "WARNINGS AND RISKS", with "Risks that may be associated with any CO2 laser procedure may include change of pigmentation, infection, erythema, skin induration or scarring."

The block varies between pieces. The AcuPulse W one-pager (PB-00021590 Rev A) lists "change of pigmentation, infection, erythema, skin induration, bleeding or scarring". The pitch deck (PB-0009551 Rev A, slide 49) adds "including but not limited to undesired skin burns". Use the newest block (PB-00082580 Rev A) unless regulatory says otherwise (open question 9).

## What this means for copy

- **Cleared, not approved, for the US.** Sources say "FDA cleared" and give a 510(k) substantial-equivalence record. No source calls AcuPulse FDA-approved.
- **Stay inside the indications.** The labeling is soft-tissue incision, excision, ablation, vaporization and coagulation, and ablative skin resurfacing with the scanning unit. The AcuPulse dermatology list names wrinkles, rhytids and furrows, acne scars, surgical scars, dyschromia and the other items quoted. It does not name acne, skin laxity, tightening, rejuvenation or body contouring.
- **FemTouch is the sensitive area.** The US labeling is soft tissue in medical specialties including gynecology. A Lumenis brochure states "INDICATION NOT CLEARED BY FDA" beside the vaginal health data, and the pitch deck says vulvovaginal atrophy "is not a cleared indication in the US". Do not present vaginal atrophy, vaginal rejuvenation, urinary incontinence or sexual-function outcomes as cleared uses.
- **Physicians only.** The manuals say the system is intended solely for trained physicians. Copy aimed at practices should not suggest delegating treatment to less experienced providers.
- **Expect, and say, the effects.** The manual lists erythema and edema (resolving over time), crusting up to 2 weeks, itching up to 2 weeks, transient pain and burning, and possible pigment change. Combo mode may mean longer downtime.
- **Skin type.** Darker skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-VI) carry a higher incidence and duration of hyperpigmentation; copy that claims suitability across skin types must carry that caution.
- **Carry the warnings and Rx text** on every piece, using the newest block above.
- **Do not borrow.** UltraPulse and other Lumenis CO2 lasers are separate products. Several AcuPulse sales pieces on file also describe UltraPulse, DeepFX, ActiveFX and SCAAR FX; statements about those do not transfer to AcuPulse.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **FemTouch and vaginal atrophy.** The US treatment guide (PB-00021600 Rev B, p.3-4) names "symptoms of vaginal atrophy in a menopausal transition to post menopause" as the patient population and says the system "is cleared ... in medical specialties such as gynecology". The 2025 brochure (PB-00082580 Rev A, p.6) says "INDICATION NOT CLEARED BY FDA" beside the vaginal health data, and the pitch deck says "VVA is not a cleared indication in the US". The guide is a 2020 document. Which statement governs, and may US copy mention vaginal atrophy at all?
2. **Women's Health wording.** The one-pager and brochure (PB-00021590 Rev A p.1; PB-00021600 Rev A p.6) call AcuPulse W Women's Health "non-hormonal, non-invasive, non-surgical" and "a revolutionary and life-changing solution", and say "No anesthesia or special post procedure care are required." The labeling calls the uses surgical or aesthetic ablation, the consent form describes "controlled ablation and coagulation of the mucosa", and the treatment guide gives post-treatment instructions (hydrating gel, no coital activity for 72 hours). The 2025 brochure also says "Safely address Women's Health issues related to vaginal health" (p.4) on the page before the "not cleared" note. Confirm which of these phrases are permitted.
3. **CE status and non-US markets.** The only CE statements on file are "AcuPulse is CE approved" (PB-00082580 Rev A, 2025; PB-1135190, 2018). No controlled document on file states a CE mark, MDR status or notified body for AcuPulse W, AcuScan120 or FemTouch. The AcuScan120 IFU (Feb 2026) has an EU-style serious-incident sentence but no CE statement. No non-US operator manual is on file for AcuPulse W. Is "CE approved" the right term (versus "CE marked"), and what market claims may be made?
4. **FDA clearance coverage.** The 510(k) records on file (K082809, K100415) are for the AcuPulse 30/40 ST and 40WG systems. No clearance record for AcuPulse W, AcuScan120, SurgiTouch handpieces, FemTouch or the 40AES platform is on file, yet the brochures say "AcuPulse is FDA cleared." Which clearance covers each configuration?
5. **AcuPulse W versus AcuPulse indications.** The AcuPulse W manual (Rev C, 2020) lists ENT, neurosurgery and orthopaedics and has no dermatology application list. The AcuPulse manual (Rev A, 2023) and the 2024-2026 instructions for use list podiatry, gynecology, dental and oral surgery and general and thoracic surgery, with the dermatology list. Is there a newer AcuPulse W manual, and does the dermatology list apply to AcuPulse W?
6. **FemTouch contraindications.** The list in the in-service addendum (CD-1012320 Rev B), consent form and deck is not in the AcuPulse W or AcuPulse operator manuals read. Is the addendum list part of current labeling, and is it US only?
7. **Manual revision order.** The file index treats UM-1091300 Rev L and UM-1044640 Rev G as the newest, by letter. By cover date and issuing entity, UM-1091300USBe Rev A (May 2023) and UM-1044640Be Rev C (February 2026) are newer, and UM-1091300USBe has footers reading both "Rev. A" and "Rev. B." Confirm the current revisions.
8. **Handpiece IFU numbering.** PB2780500Be Rev B and PB2780505 Rev C, and UM-20048160Be Rev B and UM-20048165 Rev B, look like the same documents re-numbered. Confirm that the new numbers replace the old.
9. **Standard risk block.** Three variants are in use (with and without "bleeding", and with "undesired skin burns"). Which is the current required text for B2B and patient pieces?
10. **Practice-facing positioning.** The pitch deck (slide 40) suggests delegating treatment to less experienced providers. The manuals say the system is intended solely for trained physicians. Confirm that this positioning is not to be repeated in copy.
11. **Handpiece IFU wording.** The extracted incisional-handpiece IFU text refers to the "Lumenis AcuPulse CO2 Laser system operator manual" while the quote shown above for that handpiece uses the SurgiTouch wording as extracted. Confirm against the PDF if the exact sentence is needed.
12. **No legal word list.** Confirm whether a Lumenis legal do's-and-don'ts list exists for AcuPulse. Words in use in sales material that no labeling text settles include "safe," "comfortable," "minimal downtime," "best," "gold standard" and "all-in-one."
