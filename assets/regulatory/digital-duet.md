## Indications for use (verbatim)

### US: US manual, UM-0001300 Rev F

**General intended uses (p.123):**

> The Lumenis Digital Duet is a fully integrated, high-performance diagnostic slit lamp and therapeutic laser delivery system. The Digital Duet is equipped with all of the standard controls and functions of a diagnostic slit lamp and is intended for examining the anterior segment of the eye, from the cornea epithelium to the posterior capsule. It aids in diagnosing diseases or trauma which affect the structural properties of the anterior segment. The Lumenis Digital Duet system is intended for use in a professional healthcare facility environment such as physician offices, clinics, limited care facilities, freestanding surgical centers, multiple treatment facilities and hospitals. Additionally, the Lumenis Digital Duet functions as an ophthalmic surgical laser, designed for performing procedures such as: photodisruption of ocular tissue using laser energy emitted by an Nd:YAG laser, for: posterior capsulotomy - discission of the eye's posterior capsule; iridotomy/iridectomy; selective laser trabeculoplasty

**Modes of operation, intended uses and indications for use (p.125):**

> SLT Mode: [...] The sole intended use is Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty. The indications for use are various forms of Open Angle Glaucoma (OAG).
>
> YAG Mode: [...] The intended uses are Posterior Capsulotomy (PC) and Laser Peripheral Iridotomy (LPI), sometimes simply referred to as "iridotomy". For PC, the indication for use is Posterior Capsule Opacification (PCO). For LPI, the indications for use are various forms of Angle Closure, including Angle Closure Glaucoma (ACG), Primary Angle Closure (PAC), and Primary Angle-Closure Suspect (PACS).

(The "[...]" stands for the technical parameter bullets of the source table: pulse duration, spot size, cone angle, wavelength, pulse structure, maximal energy.)

**Why the modes matter (p.124–125):**

> The two operational modes of the Digital Duet system, the SLT mode and the YAG mode, are fundamentally different, and are used for different intended uses and for treatment of different indications for use. It is essential for the physician to make sure that the correct operational mode is selected, according to the desired intended use and indication for use. Using an incorrect operational mode may result in serious ocular injuries.

**Selective laser trabeculoplasty, indications for use (p.126):**

> Indications for use of Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty are various forms of Open Angle Glaucoma (OAG), including: Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG); Secondary Open Angle Glaucoma (SOAG); Pigmentary Glaucoma (PG); Pseudo-exfoliation (PXF); Ocular Hyper Tension (OHT); Normal Tension Glaucoma (NTG).

**Posterior capsulotomy, indications for use (p.132):**

> Posterior capsulotomy is indicated for disruption of the posterior capsule and creating a clear visual pathway in case of Posterior Capsule Opacification (PCO) in pseudo-phakic eyes.

**Laser peripheral iridotomy, indications for use (p.141):**

> Laser Peripheral Iridotomy (hereafter referred to as LPI, or simply as "iridotomy"/"iridectomy") is indicated for treatment of Angle Closure suspect or Angle Closure Glaucoma (ACG). Performing iridotomy/iridectomy when the laser is in SLT mode may result in serious ocular injuries.

**Intended target groups (p.124):**

> The Selecta Laser Systems and accessories are intended for patients who meet the patient selection criteria, such as indications, contraindications and warnings, and who require and/or desire corrective or therapeutic surgeries on the eye. Specifically, any individual with open angle glaucoma (OAG), angle-closure glaucoma (ACG), or posterior capsule opacification (PCO), regardless of patient age or gender.

This US manual says "Selecta Laser Systems" here, although it is the Digital Duet manual (see Open questions).

**Clinical user (p.124):**

> The Selecta family of Laser Systems are prescriptive devices. As such, each system is designed to be used by physicians who are trained in the safe handling and operation of the system during their certification studies.

**Optional LaserLink S accessory (p.123–124):**

> If an optional LaserLink S delivery device is attached to the Digital Duet system along with a compatible Lumenis 532 nm photocoagulator laser, such as the Smart532, the Digital Duet functions solely as a standard diagnostic slit lamp and all photo-disruptor and photo-stimulator functions are disabled. The LaserLink S laser delivery adapter is used for treatments specifically cleared for the compatible laser photocoagulator.

### Non-US: Non-US manual, UM-0001301 Rev E

The indications for SLT mode, YAG mode, PC and LPI use the same words as the US manual. The non-US text is quoted where it differs in a way that matters for copy.

**Laser peripheral iridotomy, indications for use (section 4.4.1, p.140):**

> Laser Peripheral Iridotomy (hereafter referred to as LPI, or simply as "iridotomy")/iridectomy is indicated for treatment of Angle Closure suspect or Angle Closure Glaucoma (ACG). Performing iridotomy/iridectomy when the laser system is in SLT mode may result in serious ocular injuries.

**Posterior capsulotomy (sections 4.3 and 4.3.1, p.131):**

> Posterior Capsulotomy (PC) is performed using the YAG mode only, and the target is the posterior capsule in pseudo-phakic patients. Performing PC when the laser system is in SLT mode may result is serious ocular injuries. [...] Posterior capsulotomy is indicated for disruption of the posterior capsule and creating a clear visual pathway in case of Posterior Capsule Opacification (PCO) in pseudo-phakic eyes.

(The non-US manual adds the "SLT mode" injury sentence to the PC section; the US manual has it only for SLT, in the mode summary, and for LPI. The typo "result is" is in the source.)

**SLT access routes (section 4.2.2 "Method", p.124–125). This is the main market difference:**

> In the SLT procedure, the TM can be accessed via one of two different ways: the traditional SLT, where the laser beam accesses the TM via the anterior chamber (ab interno) (the traditional SLT procedure); the Direct SLT, also referred to as Trans-scleral SLT, where the TM is accessed via the peri-lumbus area and through the sclera

The US manual's general intended uses (p.123) mention the same two routes in one sentence ("either ab-interno (by using a gonioscopy contact lens), or directly through the sclera") but has no separate direct or trans-scleral section.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

### US manual, UM-0001300 Rev F

**SLT (p.129):**

> SLT is contraindicated in patients with neovascular glaucoma and angle closure glaucoma.

**Posterior capsulotomy (p.134):**

> As in all surgery, there are risks involved and use of the laser may be contraindicated for patients with certain pre-existing ocular pathologies. Objective assessment of potential patients for these procedures must be performed in light of the risks. The following represent contraindications for posterior capsulotomy: Pre-existing ocular pathologies including Corneal edema that interferes with visualization of the capsule, Diffused haze of the aqueous humor, and Extensive corneal dystrophy. Chronically elevated intraocular pressure (IOP), especially when uncontrollable under medication. Eyes with no potential visual function. Patients with glass posterior chamber intraocular lenses (IOL), except those patients whose medical condition precludes invasive surgery. Variation in IOL material and geometry may affect YAG procedural parameters and clinical success. Consult the IOL implant packaging and/or the IOL manufacturer for special considerations.

**Laser peripheral iridotomy (p.142):**

> The following represent contraindications for LPI: Eyes with opacities of the media such that the iris cannot be adequately visualized. Eyes without a pupillary block component to their glaucoma. Eyes with a glass IOL. Variations in IOL material and geometry may affect YAG procedural parameters and clinical success. Consult the IOL implant packaging and/or the IOL manufacturer for special considerations.

### Non-US manual, UM-0001301 Rev E

SLT (section 4.2.5, p.128) and LPI (section 4.4.5, p.141) contraindications use the same words as the US manual. The PC list differs in one phrase (section 4.3.5, p.133):

> Chronically elevated intraocular pressure (IOP), especially when unable to control with medication

(US Rev F: "especially when uncontrollable under medication"). The other three PC items are the same, apart from capitalization.

## Warnings, risks and precautions (verbatim)

### US manual, UM-0001300 Rev F

**SLT risks and complications (p.129–130):**

> Risks of SLT include excess energy (eye damage), inadvertent exposure (eye damage), incorrect targeting (eye damage), inadvertent contact with live electrical sources during maintenance (electrocution), overheating/fire (burns or smoke inhalation), unintended biological effect/biocompatibility/infection (physical injury), misalignment of treatment (eye damage), and inadequate cybersecurity (eye damage, ineffective treatment, or incorrect clinical decisions). In addition, since SLT treatment is a sub-threshold and does not leave visible marks, another risk of SLT treatment is the use of insufficient energy and ineffective treatment.
>
> The complication rate of SLT is very low. However, as with any surgical procedure, complications and adverse events may occur. Below is a list of complications and adverse events that may be related to SLT: Anterior chamber inflammation; IOP spikes; Conjunctivitis; Pain/discomfort; Blurred vision; Corneal edema; Corneal lesion; Headache; Peripheral anterior synechia. The most common complications are mild anterior chamber inflammation (between 0.5% to 89%) and IOP spikes (between 4.5% and 6%).

**SLT warnings and precautions (p.131):**

> The following precautions are suggested when using the Digital Duet/ Digital Trio for SLT: To reduce the risk of damage to non-targeted tissues, the treatment energy must be the minimal energy necessary to perform the treatment. Caution should be exercised when using pulse energies exceeding 1.4 mJ. Avoid blood vessels present in the angle due to the risk of hemorrhage. Exercise caution when treating patients with pre-existing anterior chamber inflammation (e.g., uveitis and iritis), since the procedure itself may induce a mild, anterior chamber inflammatory response. Ocular surgery should be performed only when the structures to be treated, and the aiming beam, can be clearly visualized. Lasing should be performed only when the tissue of interest is visualized through the slit lamp binoculars. To prevent IOP spikes, it is recommended to administer topical Apraclonidine Hydrochloride (0.5% or 1%) or Brimonidine (0.1%), before (30 to 60 minutes) or immediately after the procedure.

**YAG mode, posterior capsulotomy warnings (p.140) and LPI warnings (p.146):**

> Unlike other lasers commonly used in ophthalmology (argon, krypton, dye lasers) which rely on thermal effects, the 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser is a cutting or disrupting instrument capable of damaging any tissue or structure on which the beam is focused.

> Isolated cases of foveal burns or retinal detachments were reported when a user mistakenly chose the SLT mode instead of the YAG mode, in cases where the intended use was PC. (PC risks, p.135)

**General laser warnings and the physician caution (p.6):**

> WARNING - Use of controls or adjustments or performance of procedures other than those specified herein may result in hazardous laser radiation exposure.
>
> CAUTION - U.S. federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.
>
> CAUTION – Lumenis medical lasers and laser delivery systems are intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments.

The same federal-law caution is repeated in the symbol table (p.17): "Caution: U.S. federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician".

### Non-US manual, UM-0001301 Rev E

The risk, complication and precaution sections match the US manual in substance, with these differences that matter for copy:

- **Photophobia and trans-scleral SLT.** The non-US list of isolated SLT side effects adds photophobia ("choroidal effusion, hyphema, diffuse lamellar keratitis, refractive shifts, photophobia, macular edema", section 4.2.5, p.129). The non-US text on direct SLT says: "Since direct SLT is performed without a gonioscopy lens, there is a risk of accidentally exposing the iris, or pupil if the patient moves the eye during treatment. Such inadvertent exposure could result in harm to the iris or retina, including macular burn, peripheral anterior synechia, and photophobia. These risks can be minimized by applying a corneal shield prior to the laser procedure." (section 4.2.2, p.126). It also says that for direct SLT "finding the sub-threshold energy by titration is not possible, since gonioscopy is not employed".
- **"must" for "should".** Many instructions that say "should" in the US manual say "must" in the non-US manual (for example, "Caution must be exercised when using pulse energies exceeding 1.4 mJ"). Do not move wording between the two markets.
- **Physician caution.** The non-US manual also prints "CAUTION - U.S. federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician." (p.7) and "CAUTION – Lumenis medical lasers and laser delivery systems are intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments."

## Required disclaimers on promotional pieces (verbatim)

**"Rx ONLY" and risk statement, product presentation PB-00059371 Rev A (slide 23) and Duet and Trio brochure PB-00022530 Rev D (p.6, same text):**

> Rx ONLY
>
> Risk and warnings: Digital Duet/Trio are intended for use by trained physicians. For all indications, use is contraindicated when media opacities interfere with clear visualization. Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty is contraindicated for eyes with neovascular or angle-closure glaucoma; risks include but not limited to iritis and transient IOP rise. Posterior Capsulotomy is contraindicated for eyes with extensive corneal dystrophy and chronically elevated IOP; risks include but not limited to include IOP rise, rupture of the hyaloid face, retinal damage, and uveitis. Iridotomy is contraindicated for eyes without pupillary block; risks include but not limited to include transient elevation of IOP, hyphemia, and damage to the lens/retina/choroid. Inadvertent photocoagulation with Smart532, especially close to the fovea or the optic nerve head, can cause permanent visual loss. Major retinal vessels should be avoided. For a complete list of contraindications and risks, refer to the operator manuals.

(The PB-00022530 Rev D sentence for PC reads the same; the Smart532 sentence applies to Digital Trio only.)

YAG: contraindicated for eyes with corneal pathologies and chronically elevated IOP. Risks include IOP rise, macular edema and retinal detachment. ## Legacy devices: Selecta Duet and Selecta Trio (keep separate)

Selecta Duet and Selecta Trio are the predecessors of Digital Duet and Digital Trio. Their manuals use the same indication wording for SLT, PC and LPI, but they are different devices with their own control IDs, and their copy rules must be checked against their own manuals. Do not use "Digital" and "Selecta" names interchangeably, and do not apply legacy claims or text to Digital Duet.

How the manual defines the legacy configurations (Selecta Duet US manual UM-20058241 Rev E, introduction):

> The Selecta Duet/Trio ophthalmic laser systems includes the following configurations: Selecta Duet — a combined 1064 nm photodisruptor (YAG mode) and a 532 nm photostimulator (SLT mode); Selecta Trio — a Selecta Duet and a LaserLink S with a compatible Lumenis 532 nm photocoagulator.

## What this means for copy

- **Two modes, two sets of indications.** SLT mode is for open-angle glaucoma only (including ocular hypertension and normal tension glaucoma). YAG mode is for posterior capsulotomy in pseudo-phakic eyes with PCO and for LPI in angle closure, primary angle-closure suspect and angle-closure glaucoma. Never describe SLT as a treatment for angle-closure glaucoma (contraindicated) and never describe YAG mode as a glaucoma lowering treatment.
- **The goal of SLT is lowering eye pressure.** The manual states "The goal of SLT is to reduce the Intra-Ocular Pressure (IOP)" and "The precise mechanism of action is not completely understood." Do not state the mechanism as settled or promise a cure.
- **Physician-only.** Every source says trained physicians. Do not present Digital Duet to patients as something they choose or operate.
- **Risks travel with the product.** Promotional pieces carry "Rx ONLY" and the risk statement. Patient pieces carry the patient risk statement. A piece that names SLT benefits without the contraindications omits required text.
- **Keep the market straight.** Use US Rev F wording for US copy and non-US Rev E wording for non-US copy. Direct (trans-scleral) SLT has a separate section and extra risks only in the non-US manual.
- **Regulatory status.** The only status word in a controlled piece is "FDA-cleared" about SLT, in a patient brochure. No source on file says "approved" for Digital Duet. Use "FDA-cleared" only as the brochure does until regulatory confirms the device-level wording.
- **Digital Trio.** Only offered per regional regulatory approval. Do not show Trio (Smart532) features in a piece for a market where that approval has not been confirmed.

## Do's and don'ts

Derived only from the quotes above. Anything the quotes do not settle is under Open questions, not here.

| Topic | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A fully integrated diagnostic slit lamp and therapeutic laser delivery system that functions as an ophthalmic surgical laser (Nd:YAG photodisruption and selective laser trabeculoplasty) | Describe it as a glaucoma treatment only, or as a photocoagulator (photocoagulation needs the optional LaserLink S with a separate laser, and then Digital Duet laser functions are disabled) |
| SLT | SLT for various forms of open-angle glaucoma, including POAG, SOAG, pigmentary glaucoma, pseudo-exfoliation, ocular hypertension and normal tension glaucoma | SLT for angle-closure glaucoma or neovascular glaucoma (contraindicated) |
| Purpose | "The goal of SLT is to reduce the Intra-Ocular Pressure (IOP)" | Promise a cure, or state the mechanism of action as fully understood |
| Posterior capsulotomy | Disrupting the posterior capsule and creating a clear visual pathway for PCO in pseudo-phakic eyes (YAG mode) | PC for phakic eyes, or for eyes with extensive corneal dystrophy or chronically elevated IOP (contraindicated) |
| LPI | Treatment of angle closure suspect or angle closure glaucoma (YAG mode) | LPI for eyes without a pupillary block component (contraindicated) |
| Mode safety | Name the mode with the procedure: SLT mode for SLT, YAG mode for PC and LPI | Suggest a procedure can be done in the other mode ("may result in serious ocular injuries") |
| User | "Trained physicians" or "intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments" | Imply use by non-physicians or by patients |
| Risks | Say complications are possible and list the required risk statement | "No side effects", "risk-free", "painless for everyone". The manual lists pain/discomfort and the brochure says only that "the majority of patients describe it as painless" |
| Regulatory status | "FDA-cleared" for SLT only where the brochure's wording is reused, with the source | "FDA-approved" for Digital Duet (no source on file); any CE or MDR statement for Digital Duet (none on file) |
| Disclaimers | Print "Rx ONLY" and the risk statement on practice pieces; print the patient risk statement on patient pieces | Omit them, or shorten the contraindications |
| Trio | Say Digital Trio is "only offered for sale per regional regulatory approval in the relevant jurisdiction" | Advertise Digital Trio, Smart532 or "three lasers" in a market without confirmed approval |
| Legacy products | Use Selecta Duet and Selecta Trio names only for the legacy devices and their own manuals | Reuse Selecta text, claims or the 2020 brochure for Digital Duet |
| Exclusivity | Use only with a substantiated source ("first and only digital SLT+YAG device" and "we invented it" appear in PB-00022530 Rev D and PB-00059371 Rev A with no reference) | State "first and only" or "invented SLT" as fact in new copy without that source |

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **No device-level clearance record.** No document on file gives a 510(k) number, clearance date or CE certificate for Digital Duet (or Digital Duet 2.0). The patient brochure says SLT is "FDA-cleared" and "FDA-cleared for more than 20 years", which is about the SLT procedure or technology, and LiGHT paper text on file (Lancet 2019) says SLT "received US FDA approval in 2001". Confirm the cleared indications and the correct word ("cleared") for Digital Duet itself, and whether "FDA-cleared" may be used in patient pieces.
2. **Indication wording differs by document.** The US manual, non-US manual and presentation agree on SLT, PC and LPI. The patient brochure says SLT is "for lowering eye pressure in patients with open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension", which is narrower than the manual's list (POAG, SOAG, PG, PXF, OHT, NTG) and does not mention normal tension or pigmentary glaucoma. The Rev A brochure said only "open-angle glaucoma". Confirm which phrasing patient pieces may use.
3. **Direct (trans-scleral) SLT in the US.** The non-US manual has a separate direct SLT section with its own risks (photophobia, iris or retina harm, corneal shield). The US manual mentions it only inside one sentence of the intended uses. Confirm whether direct SLT may be mentioned in US copy.
4. **Media opacities as a contraindication for all indications.** PB-00022530 Rev D and PB-00059371 Rev A say "For all indications, use is contraindicated when media opacities interfere with clear visualization." The manuals list media opacities only as an LPI contraindication, and corneal edema or haze for PC; the SLT contraindication in the manuals is neovascular and angle-closure glaucoma only. Confirm which wording is correct.
