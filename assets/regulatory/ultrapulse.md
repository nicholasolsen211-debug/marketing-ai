# UltraPulse regulatory language and do's and don'ts

## Scope: models and divisions

- **UltraPulse Alpha (written "ULTRApulse Alpha" in the manuals) is the current model.** It is covered by the operator manual UM-2000100, which exists in two market versions (US and EU/other). Everything in Parts A, B and C below is about the Alpha unless a line says otherwise.
- **UltraPulse / UltraPulse Encore / SurgiTouch are the older models.** Their operator manual (0637-129-01 Rev H, 2017) and FDA records are in Part D only. Do not carry older-model wording, specialties or numbers over to the Alpha.
- **Two divisions use the same device.** The operator manual is one document and is the only source of indications, so it is quoted once in Part A. Part B is the Aesthetics division (skin). Part C is the Eye Care division (eyelid and periorbital, "CO2"). Each part has its own do's and don'ts. Nothing in Part B may be reused in Part C, or the other way round, unless the Part A manual text allows it.
- Dry eye: the manual lists "Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (dry eyes)" as a **complication** of blepharoplasty. UltraPulse is not a dry eye product.

## Part A. UltraPulse Alpha device labeling (applies to both divisions)

All quotes in Part A are from the Alpha operator manual. Bullets are reformatted as dashes; the wording is unchanged. "US" is UM-2000100ENUS Rev B; "EU" is UM-2000100EU Rev B. Where the two manuals use the same words, one quote is given with both page numbers.

### A1. Intended purpose, intended use (US p.302; EU p.247, same wording)

> CO2 laser, intended for surgical or aesthetic applications requiring the ablation, vaporization, excision, incision or coagulation of soft tissue

> ULTRApulse Alpha system, is intended for use in surgical or aesthetic applications requiring the ablation, vaporization, excision, incision or coagulation of soft tissue in medical specialties including: Dermatology and plastic surgery (Aesthetic), Podiatry, Gynecology, General and thoracic surgery, Dental and oral surgery and genitourinary surgery

### A2. Indications for use, general (US p.302-303; EU p.247)

US:

> The ULTRApulse Alpha CO2 Laser System, Delivery Devices and Accessories (members of the ULTRApulse CO2 Laser Systems Family) are indicated for use in surgical or aesthetic applications requiring: ablation, vaporization, excision, incision, or coagulation of soft tissue in medical specialties including:
> - Dermatology and Plastic Surgery
> - Podiatry
> - Gynecology
> - General Surgery
> - General and Thoracic Surgery
> - Dental and Oral Surgery
> - Genitourinary Surgery

> The FemTouch tips and FemX handpiece when used in conjunction with the ULTRApulse Alpha CO2 Laser System are indicated for incision, excision, ablation, vaporization and coagulation of body soft tissues in medical specialties. This includes aesthetic (dermatology and plastic surgery), podiatry, otolaryngology (ENT), gynaecology, neurosurgery, orthopaedics, general and thoracic surgery. The use with the scanning unit is indicated for ablative skin resurfacing.

EU: the same first paragraph and specialty list as the US text. **The FemTouch/FemX paragraph does not appear in the EU manual.**

> The ULTRApulse Alpha is indicated for use in specific surgical and aesthetics applications, as detailed in this chapter. Read and comprehend all of the following contraindications, warnings, precautions, and recommendations, as well as indications and safety considerations for the appropriate specialties.

### A3. Intended users and target population

US p.304 and EU p.249:

> The ULTRApulse Alpha Laser System is intended to be used by physicians trained on the use of CO2 laser systems, in an operating room or clinical facility.

EU p.249 only ("Intended Target Population"; there is no such heading in the US manual):

> The target population of the ULTRApulse Alpha System is candidates for surgical or aesthetic procedures requiring ablation, vaporization, excision, incision or coagulation of soft tissue in medical specialties including: Dermatology and plastic surgery (Aesthetics), Podiatry, Gynecology, General and thoracic surgery, Dental and oral surgery and Genitourinary surgery.

### A4. Rx / prescription text (as printed)

> Caution
> The system shall only be used according to local regulation in each territory.
> In US only: US Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.

Title page of both manuals.

> Caution
> In the USA, federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a licensed healthcare practitioner in the state that this product is prescribed.

US p.21; EU p.19.

> In the USA: Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician

Regulatory label table, US p.43; EU p.41. The same sentence with the caution heading appears in the US FemTouch chapter (p.147).

The US manual uses both "physician" and "licensed healthcare practitioner". See Open questions.

### A5. General contraindications (US p.304; EU p.249, same wording)

> - Do not use the ULTRApulse Alpha on hard tissues, such as bone or teeth.
> - Do not use the ULTRApulse Alpha for cutting or ablating dense, healthy bone or bone marrow (for example, hard palate and mandible).
> - Do not use the ULTRApulse Alpha on vessels greater than 0.5 mm in diameter, as hemostasis may not be effective and ensure the immediate availability of other surgical instruments for coagulation (i.e., electrocautery, graspers, sutures, etc.) to control hemostasis is strongly recommended.
> - Do not use the ULTRApulse Alpha where a clinical procedure is precluded by anesthesia requirements, site access, or other general operative considerations.

### A6. General warnings, notes and cautions (US p.303-305; EU p.248-250, same wording)

> Lumenis has not evaluated the use of CO2 lasers for the treatment of pregnant or nursing women.

> User should exercise caution when treating patients with metal implants or active implanted devices such as pacemakers.

> Patients with any of the following medical conditions should be treated with caution and per the physician's discretion: uncontrolled systemic diseases such as diabetes, epilepsy, autoimmune disorders, known or unknown skin conditions (such as vitiligo, psoriasis, open wounds) or congestive heart disease.

> Treatment in facial areas that were previously treated with Botox or fillers within the past 3 months should be carefully considered and performed under physician discretion.

> There are risks of excessive thermal injury, ulceration, scarring, edema, excessive bleeding, and infection - associated with any CO2 laser procedure. These risks may affect the tissue's structure and functionality. Therefore appropriate use of the CO2 laser, as well as appropriate pre -and post-procedure care should always be practiced.

> As with conventional non-laser surgery, there is no guarantee that treatment with the CO2 laser will entirely eliminate any disease/lesion/condition. Repeat treatment or alternative therapies subsequently may be required.

> The use of a laser instrument for an application is at the physician's discretion, except in cases where the application has been specifically contraindicated.

### A7. Ocular protection and periorbital treatment (US p.28-29; EU p.26-27, same wording)

> Because CO2 laser energy is absorbed by water, the cornea and sclera are the predominant ocular structures at risk for injury. These can suffer irreversible damage and scarring as a result of direct or indirect exposure.

> All personnel in the treatment room, including patients, must wear protective eyewear when the CO2 laser is in use.

> For facial and periorbital treatments (full or partial), always protect the patient with dulled, metal eye shields, as severe and irreversible eye damage and/or scarring may occur from direct or indirect exposure to the treatment beam.

> Users should exercise caution when treating patients who underwent eye laser surgery. The cornea might be too sensitive after eye laser surgery hence treatment should be under the physician discretion.

> For treatments within the orbital rim, always insert corneal eye shields. Insertion, removal and sterilization procedures to be followed as per manufacturer's guidelines.

> Serious tissue damage can occur as a result of incorrect treatment settings. Lumenis recommends performing a preliminary patient examination to assess the tissue's response prior to the first complete session. This will indicate if the patient can tolerate the treatment without developing adverse effects.

### A8. Clinical benefits statement (US p.316; EU p.261)

> The ULTRApulse Alpha is intended for a variety of aesthetic, dermatologic and surgical indications, specifically skin resurfacing, soft tissue vaporization and coagulation and soft tissue removal by incision and/or excision. With its proven effectiveness in the treatment of various medical conditions, the contribution of the ULTRApulse Alpha is in improving health, surgical and aesthetic symptoms.

### A10. Dermatology and Plastic Surgery contraindications (US p.307; EU p.252)

> Patients should not be considered for laser skin resurfacing procedures if they:
> - Have taken isotretinoin (e.g., Accutane®) within the past 12 months.
> - Have a history of keloid formation.
> - Have a history of poor wound healing.
> - Demonstrate excessive or unusually prolonged erythema, hyper- or hypopigmentation upon laser test patching.

### A11. Complications and expected sequelae, dermatology and plastic surgery (US p.308-310; EU p.253-255)

> Complications include:
> - Scarring
> - Ulceration
> - Persistent edema
> - Infection
> - Persistent erythema
> Expected Sequelae include:
> - Erythema that resolves over time.
> - Swelling/edema that resolves over time.

Skin resurfacing warnings (US p.309-310; EU p.254-255):

> The incidence and duration of post-operative hyperpigmentation and pain may be higher in patients with darker skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-VI) than in patients with lighter skin types (Fitzpatrick I-III). Therefore, careful assessment of patient skin type and laser test-patching is recommended prior to treatment.

> Temperature increase due to laser treatment in patients with previous history of herpes viruses (HV) may increase the likelihood of HV recurrence.

> To ensure proper healing of the treated area and the likelihood of hyperpigmentation, patients should avoid unprotected exposure to sunlight.

> Because clinical outcome is dependent on a variety of factors such as patient skin type, wrinkle severity, pre- and post-operative patient care and physician technique, specific parameters are not recommended by Lumenis.

> Complications may include:
> - Hypo or Hyperpigmentation
> - Scarring that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment.
> - Induration that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment.
> - Formation of fibrotic tissue that generally resolves over time with steroid treatment.
> - Formation of milia that resolves over time.
> - Reactivation of herpes simplex.
> - Acne flare-up that generally resolves over time by itself or with anti-acne medication if persistent.
> - Contact irritant dermatitis to post-operative topical agents (primarily antibiotics).

> Expected side-effects may include:
> - Transient pain that is observed immediately post-operative, and generally resolves quickly.
> - Transient burning sensation that is observed immediately post-operative, and generally resolves quickly.
> - Crusting that is observed immediately post-operative up to two weeks post-operative.
> - Itching that generally resolves within the first two weeks post-operative.
> - Textural change that is generally observed between 2 and 12 weeks post-operative, and resolves over time.
> - Sensation of tightness that resolves over time.

## Part B. Aesthetics division (skin)

### B1. Indication for use

Use Part A: A1 and A2 (intended use and general indications), A9 (Dermatology and Plastic Surgery applications) and A10 (contraindications). The US and EU wording is the same for these items; the US manual adds the FemTouch/FemX paragraph in A2.

### B2. Required warnings and Rx text to travel with copy

Use A4, A5, A6, A10 and A11. The Alpha brochure warning block (Rev C, 2023, "WARNINGS AND RISKS") is the short form used on marketing pieces:

> CO2 lasers are intended solely for use by professionals trained in the use of the Carbon Dioxide laser (10.6 μm) wavelength. Incorrect treatment settings or misuse of the technology can present risk of serious injury to patient and operating personnel. Risks that may be associated with any CO2 laser procedure may include change of pigmentation, infection, erythema, skin induration or scarring. Read and understand the CO2 systems and accessories operator manuals for a complete list of intended use, contraindications, and risks. The use of Lumenis® CO2 laser is contraindicated where a patient has taken Accutane (Isotretinoin) within the past 6-12 months, has a history of keloid formation and demonstrate excessive or unusually prolonged erythema.

(PB-00054170 Rev C; the guidelines CD-02032021 Rev B p.17 give a version that says "physicians trained" and "Rx ONLY".) **This short form differs from the manual in two places** (professionals vs physicians; 6-12 months vs 12 months). See Open questions 1 and 2.

### B3. Consumer "FDA cleared" wording used on Alpha trifolds (marketing statements, not regulatory records)

- Air Brush trifold, PB-00054190 Rev A (2023): "FDA cleared for treating wrinkles, fine lines and dyschromia"
- Acne scar trifold, PB-00054200 Rev A (2023): "FDA cleared for treating acne scars"

Both trifolds also say, in "Is it right for me?": "CO2 laser procedures are not suitable for patients who have taken Accutane® (Isotretinoin) within the past 6-12 months or had a history of keloid formation. Risks may include change of pigmentation, infection, redness, textural changes, or scarring. Be sure to consult your physician regarding treatment options available and related contraindications and risks based on your individual concerns and medical history."

No FDA clearance letter, 510(k) number or summary for the Alpha is on file, so these clearance statements could not be checked against a regulatory record (see Open questions 3).

## Part C. Eye Care division (eyelid and periorbital, "CO2")

### C1. Indication for use that applies to eyelid and periorbital work

There is no separate ophthalmic indication in the manual. The two lines that name the eyelid are, verbatim, in the Dermatology and Plastic Surgery list (A9). Quoted again here so the Eye Care reader finds them:

> Laser incision and/or excision of soft tissue, for the performance of upper and lower eyelid blepharoplasty.

> Xanthelasma palpebrarum.

(US p.307; EU p.252.) Laser skin resurfacing for "Wrinkles, rhytids and furrows (including fine lines and texture irregularities)" is also in A9. The manual does not use the words "periocular resurfacing" or "periorbital resurfacing" in its indications; it refers to "facial and periorbital treatments (full or partial)" only in the eye-shield warning (A7). Whether the Vision brochure's "periocular resurfacing" may be described as skin resurfacing under the A9 line is an open question (Open questions 4).

### C2. Warnings that Eye Care copy must respect

Use A7 in full (ocular protection, metal eye shields for facial and periorbital treatment, corneal eye shields within the orbital rim, caution after eye laser surgery), plus A4, A5, A6 and A10. Blepharoplasty complications and expected sequelae (US p.308-309; EU p.253-254, same wording):

> Blepharoplasty
> Complications include:
> - Ectropion
> - Asymmetry of eyelids.
> - Fold release (Asian eyelids).
> - Wound dehiscence.
> - Post-operative bleeding.
> - Hematoma
> - Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (dry eyes).
> - Suture abscesses that resolve without treatment.
> - Allergic reaction to surgical suture material.
> Expected side-effect include:
> - Minimal bruising and swelling (as compared to cold steel).
> - Minimal ecchymosis (as compared to cold steel).
> - Reduced intraoperative and/or post-operative pain (as compared to cold steel).
> - Slightly reduced healing time (as compared to cold steel).
> - Conjunctivitis (usually transient).
> - Ptosis (usually transient).

The Vision brochure (PB-00054171 Rev A, 2026) carries this short warning block:

> CO2 lasers are intended solely for use by professionals trained in the use of the Carbon Dioxide laser (10.6 μm) wavelength. Incorrect treatment settings or misuse of the technology can present risk of serious injury to patient and operating personnel. Risks that may be associated with any CO2 laser procedure may include change of pigmentation, infection, erythema, skin induration or scarring. Read and understand the CO2 systems and accessories operator manuals for a complete list of intended use, contraindications, and risks. The use of Lumenis® CO2 laser is contraindicated where a patient has taken Accutane (Isotretinoin) within the past 6-12 months, has a history of keloid formation and demonstrates excessive or unusually prolonged erythema.

The same two differences from the manual apply as in B2.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Who may use or be named as the operator.** The manual says "physicians trained on the use of CO2 laser systems" (A3) and the label says "physician" (A4). The same US manual says "licensed healthcare practitioner" on p.21 and, for the FemTouch chapter, "Laser surgical procedures should be performed only by individuals adequately trained in laser surgery and clinical use of CO2 lasers." The Alpha brochure and the Vision brochure say "professionals trained in the use of the Carbon Dioxide laser (10.6 μm) wavelength". The older guidelines (CD-02032021 Rev B) and the 2019 and older pieces say "physicians trained". Which term is current for marketing? Not resolved here.
2. **Isotretinoin interval.** The Alpha manuals (A10) say "within the past 12 months". The Alpha brochure, trifolds and Vision brochure say "within the past 6-12 months". The brochure also lists "excessive or unusually prolonged erythema" as a contraindication without the manual's qualifier "upon laser test patching". The manual wording was used above; marketing wording needs correction or confirmation.
3. **No Alpha FDA clearance record on file.** The Alpha manuals do not state a 510(k) number or clearance. "FDA cleared for ..." statements on PB-00054190, PB-00054200 and PB-00054210 could not be checked. The only FDA records on file are K022060 (Encore, 2002) and K030147 (SurgiTouch, 2003); K151331 is named in PB-00009100 but the document is not on file. The Product Master and Message Matrix also use counts of cleared indications ("over 30", "34", "over 100"); the Product Master is confidential, and none of the counts could be reconciled with the manual's list. Please supply the Alpha 510(k) record and the approved clearance wording.
4. **"Periocular" or "periorbital resurfacing" in Eye Care copy.** The Vision brochure (PB-00054171 Rev A) uses "periocular resurfacing", "one platform. All around ocular treatment" and "periocular treatments". The manual's indications do not use these words; its only periorbital text is the eye-shield warning. Confirm whether "laser skin resurfacing for wrinkles, rhytids and furrows" covers periorbital skin, and whether "ocular treatment" must be removed (the manual's ocular text is injury protection only).
5. **Ophthalmology and oculoplastic surgery as a specialty.** The manuals say "in medical specialties including: Dermatology and plastic surgery (Aesthetic), Podiatry, ..."; ophthalmology is not named. Confirm whether Eye Care copy aimed at ophthalmologists and oculoplastic surgeons may describe the eyelid indications as above without calling them an ophthalmic indication.
6. **EU/other market claims.** The EU Alpha manual (UM-2000100EU) carries no CE statement or notified body number in the extracted text. The only "CE approved" wording on file is on the 2017 SCAAR FX brochure for the older model. There is no source on file to support an EU conformity claim for the Alpha, and the EU manual lacks the FemTouch/FemX paragraph that the US manual has.
7. **US versus EU manual.** Both are "Revision B" (US: Copyright 2023, October 2024; EU: Copyright 2025-2026, March 2026). The EU manual adds "Intended Target Population" and omits the FemTouch/FemX indication. The US manual has a second, shorter FemTouch indication (p.147: "aesthetic (dermatology and plastic surgery), podiatry, gynecology, general and thoracic surgery") that does not match the p.302-303 version (adds ENT, neurosurgery, orthopaedics). Confirm which is current and whether a newer US version exists.
8. **Depth claim.** Alpha materials (PB-00054170 Rev C, PB-00054171 Rev A, CD-02032021 Rev B) say up to 4 mm ablation depth in a single pulse; the older PB-00009100 Rev A and the 2019 newsletter (PB-00005470 Rev A) say up to 3.5 mm. Not a labeling matter, but the numbers differ by model and must not be mixed. The manual quotes were not checked for an official depth figure.
9. **Dry eye wording in Eye Care copy.** Confirm the standing rule that no dry eye, MGD or ocular surface claim may be made for UltraPulse.
10. **Consumer pieces.** The trifolds say "Long lasting", "dramatically improves" and "significant improvement" with no qualifier, against the manual's "no guarantee" and "specific parameters are not recommended by Lumenis". They carry the Rx and contraindication block only in the "Is it right for me?" paragraph. Confirm they are acceptable as written.
11. **What was verified.** Quotes in Parts A, B, C and D were taken from the extracted text of the files named in Sources and page numbers are the printed manual page numbers (US/EU) or the printed page of the piece. The US and EU Alpha manuals were compared by script for the quoted sentences (same wording in both unless stated). Nothing was checked against the original PDFs visually, against a live FDA database, or against any web source.
