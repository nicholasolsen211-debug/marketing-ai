# OptiPLUS regulatory language and do's and don'ts

**Read this first.** The three OptiPLUS user manuals on file state three different indications for use, one per market. Only the Canadian manual lists dry eye disease (DED) due to meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), and only "in conjunction with" OptiLIGHT and gland expression. The US manual (R4) and the EU/other manual (R00) do not mention dry eye or the eye area as an indication. The 2025 patient flyer PB-00077490 Rev B nevertheless describes meibomian gland, circulation and collagen benefits. Treat every dry eye or eye-area statement as market-specific and unresolved for the US until the regulatory owner answers the open questions at the end of this page.

## Indications for use (verbatim)

> **US, MN0203-US-Opti.R4, §3 "Indications for Use", p.14**
>
> The OptiPLUS is intended:
> - to provide topical heating to treat selected medical conditions such as for temporary relief of pain or muscle spasms and to increase local circulation on body and face.
> - to provide, with a massage device, a temporary reduction in the appearance of cellulite.

> **Canada, MN0203-CAN-Opti.R1, §3 "Indications for Use", p.14**
>
> OptiPLUS intended purpose is to develop localized heat to warm the subcutaneous tissue by means of radio frequency energy, delivered through electrodes in contact with the patient body and face.
>
> The biological effects of the OptiPLUS are linked to the increase of the local temperature of the tissues, in the different body areas. These effects bring to several clinical effects, the device indications for use are:
> - Treatment of edematous-fibrous-sclerous dermopanniculosis
> - Treatment of skin atrophy and elastosis
> - Treatment of microcirculation and venous insufficiency
> - Treatment of hyperplasia and hypertrophy of adipocytes
> - Treatment of signs and symptoms of evaporative dry eye disease (DED), also known as dry eye syndrome or lipid tear deficiency, due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD) - only when used in conjunction with Lumenis' OptiLight (Licence No.: 109995) and meibomian gland expression (MGX).

> **Canada, MN0203-CAN-Opti.R1, §3.6 "Clinical Treatment Protocol for DED due to MGD", pp.16-18**
>
> Treatment of signs and symptoms of DED due to MGD is only permitted when combined with Intense Pulsed Light (IPL) using Lumenis' OptiLIGHT and meibomian gland expression (MGX).
>
> Prior to OptiPLUS treatment, IPL treatment needs to be performed using Lumenis' OptiLIGHT. For IPL treatment protocol, see OptiLIGHT user manual.
>
> The RF application treatment area is from the supraorbital foramen until the anterior lacrimal crest along the orbital rim (green C-shape). Don't treat within the orbital rim.
>
> Use of the Monopolar handpieces, with electrodes ø 20 or ø 10 for treatment of the periorbital area

> **EU/other, MN0203-EN.R00, §2.1-2.1.2, p.12**
>
> The intended purpose of the OptiPLUS is to increase the local temperature of the tissue in the treatment area by using radiofrequency stimulation for treatment of skin atrophy, elastosis, edematous-fibrous-sclerous dermopanniculosis and reduction of adipose tissue through lipolysis.
>
> Indications for use with intended medical purposes: The OptiPLUS is indicated for: Treatment of skin atrophy, elastosis, edematous-fibrous-sclerous dermopanniculosis and through thermal activation of fibroblasts leading to production of new collagen and elastin.
>
> Indications for use without intended medical purposes: The OptiPLUS is indicated for: Reduction of adipose tissue through lipolysis.

> **EU/other, MN0203-EN.R00, §2.2, p.12**
>
> The OptiPLUS is intended to be used in the professional environment by trained, licensed physicians. The OptiPLUS is a prescription device, and its use is restricted to licensed physician [...]

> **US, MN0203-US-Opti.R4, §3.1, p.14**
>
> OptiPLUS must be used in healthcare settings under the supervision of medical personnel, with knowledge of operation and all applications related to the use of the devices.

The Canadian manual's §3.1 and §3.2 read identically to the US ones. Section 2.1 (safety information) and §§3.2-3.5 are word-for-word identical in the US R4 and Canadian R1 manuals (checked by comparing the extracted text after removing page footers).

## Contraindications (verbatim)

> **US, MN0203-US-Opti.R4, §3.3, pp.14-15** (identical in Canada, MN0203-CAN-Opti.R1, §3.3, p.15)
>
> The following patients should not be treated:
> - Taking medicines that increase skin sensitivity to heat;
> - Taking medicines that change skin metabolism;
> - Cancer or pre-cancerous lesions in the treatment area;
> - Herpes simplex in the treatment area;
> - Pregnancy;
> - Pacemaker wearers;
> - Inflammatory skin conditions or other significant skin conditions;
> - Open lacerations or abrasions;
> - Chronic or cutaneous viral, fungal or bacterial diseases;
> - Active local or systemic infections;
> - Any disease in which a temperature increase is contraindicated such as coagulant disorder (including atrial fibrillation, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, venous thromboembolism, coronary artery disease, mechanical heart valves, and hypercoagulable states) and peripheral vascular disease or pertinent neurological disorders
> - Pediatric patients, unless otherwise medically indicated.
>
> OptiPLUS should only be used for treatments on intact skin; OptiPLUS should not be used on infected or damaged skin.
>
> The device is not intended to be used within the orbital rim or on the neck.
>
> Operator should avoid heating up the glands (under arm).
>
> In case of bleeding at any time during the treatment with OptiPLUS, stop immediately and remove the handpiece. Contact the customer service for more information.

> **EU/other, MN0203-EN.R00, §2.4.1, pp.14-15** (a different and longer list; the first four and the last items are quoted, see the manual for the rest)
>
> Contraindications of treatments with OptiPLUS include:
> - Presence of any metallic passive medical device or other metallic object within or on the body, including but not limited to implants, prosthetics, pacemakers, internal defibrillator, implantable cardioverter-defibrillator, nerve stimulator implant, cochlear implant or any other electronically, magnetically or mechanically activated implant
> - Any active implantable medical device or active body-worn medical device, including those with electronic components and requiring power for operation.
> - Coagulant disorders, including atrial fibrillation, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, venous thromboembolism, coronary artery disease, mechanical heart valves, and hypercoagulable states
> - History of hypertension, ischemic heart disease, valvular heart disease, congestive heart failure, and abdominal aortic aneurysm
> - [...]
> - Pregnancy
> - [...]
> - Individuals under the age of 18 years, unless otherwise medical indication
> - [...]
> - Epilepsy

## Warnings and precautions (verbatim)

> **US and Canada, §2.1 "Safety Information" (US R4 pp.9-11; Canada R1 pp.9-12)**
>
> The OptiPLUS is a medical device. It can be used for medical treatments. It must not be used for any applications that are not indicated in this document.
>
> DO NOT use this device over piercings due to potential adverse reactions of metallic particles to the RF energy.
>
> It is recommended that you avoid treatments directly over areas containing injectable fillers and toxins for one month.
>
> Because ongoing feedback by a patient during a procedure is important, avoid treating patients who have nerve insensitivity to heat anywhere in the treatment area.
>
> During the treatment, the patient will feel warmth and heat and should be encouraged to provide ongoing feedback to the individual performing the treatment. Therefore, no anesthetic (local, oral, or systemic) should be used prior to or during the treatment.
>
> For the face, recommend setting the target temperature no higher than 42°C to ensure patient comfort.
>
> DO NOT USE in patients who have electronic implants such as cardiac pacemakers without first consulting a qualified professional (e.g., cardiologist). The possibility for interference with the functionality of the electronic implant may occur, or the implant may become damaged.

> **US and Canada, §3.4 Precautions (US R4 pp.15-16; Canada R1 pp.15-16)**
>
> Patients with the following conditions should be treated with caution and per the physician's discretion: Livedo reticularis, an autoimmune vascular disease; the management may include topical corticosteroids, antihistamines and discontinuance of treatments. Erythema ab igne; when identified, treatments should be discontinued. Use of medication, herbal supplements, perfumes or cosmetics that may affect sensitivity to the treatment. Morbidly obese patients. Patients who have had prior problems with radiofrequency therapy should be carefully screened before treatment.

> **EU/other, MN0203-EN.R00, §2.4.3 Warnings, p.17**
>
> The OptiPLUS point of application shall not be near the eye, mouth, nose, ears, or genitals.

> **EU/other, MN0203-EN.R00, §2.4.4 Precautions, p.18**
>
> Stimulation should not be applied across or through the head, directly on the eyes, covering the mouth, on the front of the neck, (especially the carotid sinus), or from electrodes placed on the chest and the upper back or crossing over the heart.

## What this means for copy

Derived only from the quotes above and from the existing legal word list that follows.

- **Dry eye and MGD are Canada-only indications on file, and always paired.** Where the Canadian manual applies, the wording is treatment of signs and symptoms of evaporative DED due to MGD, "only when used in conjunction with" OptiLIGHT and meibomian gland expression. Never describe OptiPLUS as treating dry eye on its own. No dry eye indication appears in the US R4 or EU/other R00 manuals.
- **Keep the market straight.** The US, Canadian and EU/other indications differ. Do not carry the Canadian dry eye line into US or EU copy, and do not carry the EU skin atrophy, elastosis, dermopanniculosis or fat-reduction lines into US copy.
- **Eye area.** All three manuals restrict use near the eye: "not intended to be used within the orbital rim" (US, Canada), "shall not be near the eye" and "directly on the eyes" (EU/other). The Canadian protocol applies RF "along the orbital rim" and says "Don't treat within the orbital rim." Copy must not describe treatment on the eyelids or inside the orbital rim.
- **Operator.** US and Canadian manuals: "healthcare settings under the supervision of medical personnel." EU/other: "trained, licensed physicians." Do not present OptiPLUS as a home or consumer device.
- **Pain and comfort.** The manuals list burning sensation, burns, blistering and moderate pain as side effects, and say no anesthetic should be used. Do not write "painless" or imply numbing cream is used.
- **Recovery.** US and Canadian manuals say erythema and edema "typically resolve within a few hours"; the EU/other manual says "within a few days." Do not write an unqualified recovery time.
- **Regulatory status.** "FDA-cleared" is the wording used on the 2025 flyer and the existing legal list. Never "FDA-approved."

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Which indication governs dry eye in the US?** The US manual MN0203-US-Opti.R4 lists only topical heating (pain, muscle spasms, local circulation on body and face) and a temporary reduction in the appearance of cellulite. It has no dry eye, MGD or periorbital protocol. The Canadian manual MN0203-CAN-Opti.R1 (13 January 2026) lists DED due to MGD with OptiLIGHT. The EU/other manual MN0203-EN.R00 (22 January 2026) lists skin atrophy, elastosis, dermopanniculosis and fat reduction and says the point of application "shall not be near the eye." The 2025 US flyer PB-00077490 Rev B nonetheless describes improved meibomian gland function and pain relief for sensitive eyes. Confirm the US labeling position and whether a newer US manual than R4 exists.
2. **EU/other manual may be incomplete or processed.** "threat_extracted_MN0203-EN.R00 OptiPLUS.pdf" is a processed copy. Confirm it matches the released manual, and whether any EU/other manual carries a dry eye indication.
3. **FDA and Health Canada status.** No 510(k) number, De Novo number or OptiPLUS Health Canada licence number appears in any current document on file. "FDA-cleared" appears only on the flyer. The older US manual (MN0206 R0) says "Class 2." Confirm the clearance basis and whether dry eye or eye-area use is within it.
4. **Flyer versus legal list.** The 2025 flyer uses "spa-like treatment," "tightens skin," "Say goodbye to invasive procedures," "all skin types" and "no-downtime," and discusses dry eye benefits without naming OptiLIGHT. The 2023 legal list rules several of these out. Confirm whether the list has been updated since October 24, 2023, and obtain the original list for this repository.
5. **Recovery and "no-downtime."** The flyer says "no-downtime"; the manuals list burns, blistering and moderate pain as possible side effects and give recovery times of hours (US/Canada) or days (EU/other). Confirm approved recovery wording.
6. **Where "Rx Only" must appear.** The manuals carry "Rx Only / Caution: U.S. Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician." No source says which marketing channels must repeat it. Confirm.
7. **Three versions of the consent form.** PB-00070410 Rev A exists as three files with different text (one adds the sentence "For treatment on the face, some patients may experience ears feeling clogged and jaw tightness" and the eligibility question "Open lacerations or abrasions?"; the other two do not). They share the same control ID and revision, so the newest cannot be established. The form also describes the device by "reduction in the appearance in cellulite" and gives treatment spacing of "at least 1 week apart and no more than 3 weeks apart" with "4-6 treatments", while the flyer says four sessions two weeks apart. Confirm the controlled version.
8. **"Dual frequency" wording.** The legal list requires "dual frequency RF technology." The manuals describe a "FocalRF generator at 470 KHz, 1, 2, 4 and 6 MH.
