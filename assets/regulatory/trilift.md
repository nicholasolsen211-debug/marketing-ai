# triLIFT regulatory language and do's and don'ts

triLIFT is sold by two divisions. **Aesthetics** and **Eye Care** each have their own section below. Do not merge them, and do not move wording from one division's section into the other's copy.

No legal do's-and-don'ts word list for triLIFT was found among the sources. The do's and don'ts below are derived only from the quoted labeling text. They are not a legal word list.

## Currency rule used on this page

Only the newest revision on file of each controlled document is used as a current source. Where two current documents disagree, each wording is quoted separately and the conflict is listed under "Open questions". Older revisions are listed under "Superseded" and are not quoted as current.

## Aesthetics

### Indications for use and intended use (verbatim)

**US and general, user manual (the controlling labeling on file).** triLift User Manual, Part Number 11600560 ver.4 (May 2023), section 3 "Indications", pp.13-14. Section 1, p.6, repeats the same wording.

> The TriLift™ system is intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using triFX™ Energy (Applicator VO), as well as intended for use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for the non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides when using TriPollar® RF Energy (Applicators 1-3).
>
> The triLift with the DMSt ™ mode is intended for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles. triLift with the DMSt ™ mode is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind. triLift is intended to be operated by a trained professional who is present to monitor treatment.

Same manual, section 1, p.6:

> Intended settings for the system: Clinics & Hospitals.
> Intended users: Healthcare professionals

**Australia, user manual.** Part Number 11600560 ver.4, section 18 "Intended Use for Australia", p.60. Section 3 says: "IFU for Australia please refer to section 18."

> triLift in Australia is supplied for the following Intended use:
> The triLift system is intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin and use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for the non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides.
> The device is also intended for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles.

**US, 2026 professional brochure.** PB-00096753 Rev A (2026), p.6, "Indications for use". The wording is the manual's wording except for the small differences shown here: it does not say "Australia", and the last sentence ends "to monitor treatment."

> The triLift™ system is intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using triFX™ Energy (Applicator VO), as well as intended for use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for the non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides when using TriPollar® RF Energy (Applicators 1-3). The triLift with the DMSt™ mode is intended for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles. triLift with the DMSt™ mode is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind. triLift is intended to be operated by a trained professional who is present to monitor treatment.

**2026 consumer brochures (different wording, market not stated).** PB-00096116 Rev A (face) p.1 and PB-00096124 Rev A (body) p.1 carry the same "Indication For Use" block:

> The triLift Platform and its accessories are intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using VoluDerm Energy (Applicator VO). (tips 12, 100, 36, 36L, H7X7).
> The Applicator VO with XL, XL2, XL3 tip is intended for use in dermatological procedures for electrocoagulation and hemostasis. At higher energy levels greater than 62 mJ/pin, use of the Applicator VO with XL, XL2, XL3 tip is limited to Skin Types I–IV.

(Applicators 1-3).

This is not the same text as the user manual (see Open questions 1). The consumer block does not repeat the "not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases" sentence or the "trained professional" sentence.

### Contraindications (verbatim)

**Short form, 2026 professional brochure.** PB-00096753 Rev A, p.6:

> triLift is contraindicated for patients with a pacemaker, defibrillator, or any implanted electronic device, or metal implants in the treatment area.

**Full list, user manual.** Part Number 11600560 ver.4, section 4, pp.14-15:

> 4.1. General contraindications
> Patients having the following conditions should not be treated:
> • Pacemaker, defibrillator, or any implanted electronic system
> • Metal implants in the treatment area
> • Pregnancy or nursing
> • Severe concurrent illness or condition such as cancer, lupus, uncontrolled diabetes, uncontrolled seizure disorders
> • Concurrent or chronic skin disorders or lesions in the treatment area
> • Severe bleeding or vascular disorders
> • Under 18 years of age
> • Patients who cannot feel heat to give proper treatment feedback (nerve damage, etc.)
> • Those who form keloids or heal poorly
> • Injected chemical substance, threads, and synthetic fillers in the treated area.
> • Fillers, collagen, fat injections or other injected biomaterial in the treated area within three months prior to treatment and not before complete healing has occurred, Botox in the treated area within 2 weeks prior to treatment.
>
> 4.2. TriPollar RF and triFX related contraindications
> • Impaired immune system due to autoimmune diseases, immunosuppressive diseases or use of immunosuppressive medications
> • Do not treat over tattoos or permanent make-up
> • Those who have been on Accutane (Isotretinion) within the last six months
>
> 4.3. Relative contraindications (treatable at the physician's discretion):
> • History of Herpes or similar conditions that can be triggered by heat should be treated prophylactically with the appropriate medication
> • Patients taking medications, herbal preparations, food supplements or vitamins that might cause fragile skin or impaired skin healing such as prolonged steroid regime, tetracyclines, or St. John's Wort.
> • Excessively tanned skin from sun, tanning beds, or spray tans etc.
> • Use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDS, e.g., ibuprofen containing agents). Best practice would allow one week before and after treatment without use
> • Be sure that any other procedures such as surgery, laser treatments, chemical peels, injections, implants etc., are completely healed before treatment. Botox should be allowed to calm down at least 2-3 weeks before treatment, fillers 6 weeks plus, and more invasive procedures longer

### What this means for Aesthetics copy

- **The user manual's indication is the reference text.** It covers (1) skin ablation and resurfacing with the triFX applicator, (2) non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides with TriPollar RF, and (3) muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles with DMSt mode. It says DMSt mode "is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind."
- **Cellulite, body contouring, volume and "lift" do not appear in any indication text on file.** The body brochure (PB-00096124 Rev A) uses cellulite and contouring language. Its own "Indication For Use" block does not mention cellulite, and the cellulite figure is "data on file".
- **The 2026 consumer brochures name periorbital DMSt stimulation "for aesthetic purposes".** The user manual does not. Do not reuse that wording in the US or elsewhere until the conflict in Open questions 1 is resolved.
- **Eye area.** The manual says never to treat below the eyebrow or over the eye lids, eyeballs or eye socket (pp.44-46, 49).
- **Provider-administered only.** "Intended to be operated by a trained professional who is present to monitor treatment."
- **US copy carries the Rx statement.** Use the manual's CAUTION text wherever the channel requires labeling text.

### What this means for Eye Care copy

- **Stay inside the indication.** It names "transcutaneous electrical stimulation of periorbital, facial, and body muscles with DMSt ... for aesthetic purposes, including improvement of appearance." It names no disease, no dry eye, no blinking disorder and no ocular outcome.
- **Blinking.** The only quoted limit is the brochure's own: "References to blinking in this brochure relate to the stimulation of muscles involved in the blinking process."
- **The manual's eye-area warnings apply to the same device** (Aesthetics section): never treat below the eyebrow, over the eye lids, eyeballs or eye socket.
- **Study results only in study context.** PB-00094660 Rev A shows "STUDY RESULTS" with the Chelnis and Chelnis 2025 reference. Keep the reference with the figures. See the Eye Care claims file.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Which indication text is current?** The user manual (version 4, May 2023) and PB-00096753 Rev A (2026) give one text. The 2026 consumer brochures (PB-00096116, PB-00096124) and the Eye Care brochure (PB-00093457) add "transcutaneous (electrical) stimulation of periorbital, facial, and body muscles with DMSt ... for aesthetic purposes, including improvement of appearance", and the consumer brochures add an Applicator VO XL-tip paragraph on electrocoagulation and hemostasis. These are newer than the manual but are marketing pieces, not labeling. No manual or IFU for "triLIFT 2.0" is on file. Confirm the current labeling and which markets each text covers.
2. **Eye-area copy against the manual's warnings.** The manual says never to treat below the eyebrow, over the eye lids, eyeballs or eye socket, and to treat only over bone when working around the eye. Current copy says "Tighten the undereye area" (PB-00096753 Rev A p.3), "Saggy lower eyelids?" (PB-00096116 Rev A) and, for Eye Care, "Lift and Tone Around the Eyes" and "TARGET DROOPY EYE BAGS, INCOMPLETE BLINKING" (PB-00093457 Rev A). The uncontrolled Quick Reference Guide (2025) contains an "UNDER EYE AREA PROTOCOL". Confirm which wording is allowed.
3. **FDA status wording.** No source on file says "approved". "FDA CLEARED" appears only in an uncontrolled 2024 presentation, and "FDA indicated for muscle conditioning" in the 2022 deck PB-00053360 Rev A. No 510(k) number or clearance letter is on file. Confirm the status and the exact wording, including for triLIFT 2.0.
4. **Medical-condition sentence.** The manual says DMSt mode "is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind." The Eye Care brochure omits it and discusses dry-eye lid closure, "incomplete blinking" and "ocular health". Confirm whether the sentence must travel with Eye Care copy and how far dry-eye context may go.
5. **Eye Care risk text.** The Eye Care documents on file carry no contraindications or warnings. The practice brochure PB-00093456 Rev A, cited by the existing claims file, is not on file. Confirm what risk text Eye Care materials need.
6. **Operator wording.** The manual and PB-00096753 Rev A say "trained professional". The manual also says "Healthcare professionals" and "prescribed". Confirm the current wording and whether US "licensed healthcare practitioner" wording should appear.
7. **Skin types.** The consumer brochures say "No matter what your age, complexion, or skin type is" and the Eye Care brochure says "No matter your age, complexion, or skin type." The manual carries a skin type IV-VI caution, and the consumer indication block limits XL tips above 62 mJ/pin to skin types I-IV. Confirm whether the unqualified wording is allowed.
8. **Name and version.** The manual says "triLift" and uses "triFX" for the microneedle energy. The 2026 pieces say "triLIFT 2.0" and "VoluDerm Energy" for the same applicator; FibroBoost is used as a brand in the 2026 pieces and is not defined in the manual. Confirm that the manual covers triLIFT 2.0.
9. **Consumer risk text.** The 2026 consumer brochures contain no risk statement. The only consumer risk text on file is in PB-00065530 Rev A (2023). Confirm whether a newer one exists.
10. **Markets other than the US and Australia.** No CE mark, EU intended use or other market text is on file. The manual's Australian intended use omits the DMSt "not intended ... medical diseases" sentence and the "trained professional" sentence.
11. **Revision letters.** The user manual has no PB number and no copyright year. PB-00096753's printed footer shows no revision letter (the file name says Rev A). PB-00043560's printed revision is "Rav A". Confirm the control IDs and revisions.
