# Retina and glaucoma lasers regulatory language and do's and don'ts

This page records what the Lumenis operator manuals, delivery-device manuals and controlled sales pieces on file say about each retina and glaucoma laser. Check copy against the section for **each device the piece names** before using the [claims library](../claims-reference/retina-and-glaucoma-lasers.md). **Each device has its own section. Never carry one device's indications, contraindications or wording over to another**, including a laser and the delivery device or accessory that attaches to it (for example, an indication in the Vision One manual is not a Smart 532 or SmartLIO indication).

Nothing here is approval. Quotes are copied from the extracted text of the source PDFs; bullet glyphs are shown as list markers and original spelling is kept. Every piece still goes through legal and regulatory review.

## How the currency rule was applied

- Only the newest revision on file of each controlled document is used as the current statement. Older revisions are listed under [Superseded](#superseded).
- Where two current documents disagree (a US manual and an international manual, or a manual and a brochure), each wording is shown under its own market and the conflict is logged under [Open questions](#open-questions-for-the-regulatory-owner). The manual is preferred over the brochure for regulatory statements.
- Control ID and revision were taken from each document's own title page or footer, not from the automatic index. Where the two disagree, the document wins and the difference is noted.
- Documents in the `7 Archive` folder were not used as current sources.

## Smart 532

Two Smart 532 operator manuals are current for their own markets. **State each market's wording separately and do not merge them.** They disagree (see Open questions 1 and 2).

### Smart 532, US edition (UM-1151440ENBe Rev. A, Feb 2023)

Indication statement, section 1.1 (p.1-1) and 3.1 (p.3-1):

> The Smart532 Laser System is indicated for ophthalmic applications; ear, nose and throat applications; dentistry and dermatologic applications. Complete and detailed lists of the system's clinical indications appear in Appendix A of this manual – Clinical Guide.

Clinical Guide A.2 and A.2.1 (p.A-4):

> The Smart532 is intended for use in the treatment of ocular, otolaryngo-logical, dermatologic and dentistry indications as listed below.
>
> The indications for use in ophthalmology are:
> - Diabetic Retinopathy including Macular Edema or Proliferative Retinopathy
> - Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR)
> - Retinal Tear
> - Macular Edema or Proliferative Retinopathy associated with Central Retinal Vein Occlusion (CRVO) or Branch Retinal Vein Occlusion (BRVO).
> - Choroidal Neovascularization (CNV) secondary to Age-related Macular Degeneration (AMD)
> - Central Serous Chorioretinopathy (CSCR)
> - Trabeculoplasty for Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG)
> - Iridotomy / Iridoplasty for Angle-Closure Glaucoma (CAG)

The same manual lists ear, nose and throat (A.2.2), dermatologic (A.2.3) and dentistry (A.2.4) indications on pp.A-4 to A-5. Those belong to other specialties and are **not reproduced here and not for use in Eye Care copy.**

Contraindications, A.4.3 (p.A-7):

> Laser treatment is contraindicated when an appropriate procedure cannot be performed safely. This could occur when tissue targets cannot be visualized properly. Corneal opacities, cataract formation and vitreous hemorrhage can all interfere with the physician's view of target tissues, possibly leading to inadvertent photocoagulation of tissues/structures adjacent to the tissue/structure of interest. Treatment should be delayed until the ocular media problem resolves or is treated. If it is not possible, an alternative form of therapy should be considered.

Warnings, A.4.8 (p.A-9):

> - Only appropriate tissue targets should be subject to photocoagulation.
> - Attention should be paid to avoid treating major retinal vessels, as vitreous hemorrhage and serious visual loss could occur.
> - The optic nerve head should not be coagulated, as severe and permanent visual loss could occur.
> - Inadvertent macular photocoagulation, especially close to the fovea (within 300-500µm), can cause severe and permanent visual loss.
> - Excessively high power settings should not be used, as they can cause damage to tissues adjacent to the target of interest.

Adverse effects, posterior segment, A.6.1 (p.A-13):

> The most common complication is increased macular edema secondary to pan-retinal photocoagulation, usually with a concurrent decrease in visual acuity. [...] In addition, blowout hemorrhages from areas of neovascularization, particularly on the optic nerve, have been observed.

Anterior segment warnings, A.7.2 (p.A-16) and adverse effects A.7.3 (p.A-16):

> Following iridotomy or trabeculoplasty, intraocular pressure (IOP) should be closely monitored.
>
> Medical therapy should be continued following laser Trabeculoplasty
>
> A "snuff out" of the central visual acuity has been reported as a complication of an intraocular pressure spike. Patients with advanced field loss should be carefully treated and monitored. Progressive field loss following laser trabeculoplasty is possible, even in the presence of seemingly adequately controlled intraocular pressure

Required disclaimers and user statements (title page p.2, and 2.4 Caution p.2-8):

> In the US: Caution: Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.
>
> Lumenis medical lasers and laser delivery devices are intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments.

Label list, 2.11 (p.2-14): "Rx Only – Caution: Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician."

### Smart 532, international edition (UM-1151441BeEN Rev. A, Nov 2025)

Applies outside the US. Clinical Guide, A.2.1 General Intended Uses (p.A-3):

> In the posterior segment of the eye, ophthalmic procedures include focal/grid, sectoral, pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) and barrage (retinopexy). In the anterior segment of the eye, ophthalmic procedures include trabeculoplasty, iridoplasty and iridotomy.

A.2.2 Intended Users (p.A-3):

> The Smart532 Laser System and its accessories (Smart LIO and LaserLinks) are intended to be used only by physicians trained in the use of these instruments. It is intended to be used in the clinical setting of a professional healthcare facility environment such as physician offices, clinics, limited care facilities, freestanding surgical centers, multiple treatment facilities and hospitals.

A.2.3 Intended Population (p.A-3):

> The Smart532 Laser System and the accessories (Smart LIO and LaserLinks) are intended to be used to treat patients with retinal diseases, regardless of age and gender.

A.3.1 Indications for Use, Ophthalmic Posterior Segment (Retina) (p.A-8):

> The Smart532 is indicated for use in the treatment of:
> - Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) including Diabetic Macular Edema (DME) or non-Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (nPDR)
> - Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR)
> - Retinal Tear (RT) and Retinal Detachment (RD)
> - Macular Edema (ME) or Proliferative Retinopathy associated with Central Retinal Vein Occlusion (CRVO) or Branch Retinal Vein Occlusion (BRVO)

The A.3.1 list has no anterior-segment (glaucoma), choroidal neovascularization or central serous chorioretinopathy entry; the manual's only anterior-segment wording is the A.2.1 sentence above.

Contraindications, A.3.5.1 (p.A-15):

> Treatment with the Smart532 and accessories (e.g Smart LIO and LaserLinks) is contraindicated when media opacities such as corneal opacities, cataract and vitreous hemorrhage interfere with the physician's view of the retina. Laser treatment of the retina is contra-indicated in patients who have no pigmentation in the RPE cells (albino patients). In infants with Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP), treatment with the Smart532 is contraindicated as its laser energy at this wavelength may be absorbed in the tunica vasculosa lentis and trigger cataract formation.

The same section lists further contraindications for panretinal photocoagulation (pp.A-15 to A-16), starting "Grade IV or greater glial proliferation exists throughout the posterior pole of the eye".

Complications, A.3.5.3 (p.A-17), and warnings, A.3.5.4.1 (pp.A-18 to A-19):

> The most common complication is increased macular edema secondary to pan-retinal photocoagulation, usually with a concurrent decrease in visual acuity.
>
> - Only appropriate tissue targets should be subject to photocoagulation.
> - Attention should be paid to avoid treating major retinal vessels, as vitreous hemorrhage and serious visual loss could occur.
> - The optic nerve head should be avoided, as its targeting could lead to severe and permanent visual loss.
> - Inadvertent macular photocoagulation, especially close to the fovea (within 300-500µm), can cause severe and permanent visual loss. Never treat close to the fovea when using the CW mode.

General warning, A.2.4 (p.A-3):

> Complications of laser therapy are the same as complications for any other surgical procedure. Some of these complications may be serious and could result in death or permanent blindness.

Required label text, 2.x label list (PDF p.27, printed "2-13"):

> Rx Only – Caution: Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.

The same list names "Details of the manufacturer, distributor and of the authorized representative in the European Community" and the CE marking. No wording citing the EU MDR (2017/745) was found in the extracted text.

### Do's and don'ts (Smart 532)

| Topic | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Market-specific indications | Name the indication list from the edition that matches the audience: US list (p.A-4, UM-1151440ENBe) or international list (p.A-8, UM-1151441BeEN) | Mix the two lists, or present the US list (CNV secondary to AMD, CSCR, POAG trabeculoplasty, ACG iridotomy/iridoplasty) to an international audience without confirmation |
| Retinal conditions common to both | Diabetic retinopathy including macular edema, proliferative diabetic retinopathy, retinal tear, macular edema or proliferative retinopathy with CRVO or BRVO | Imply any other retinal condition |
| Anterior segment / glaucoma | For US audiences, "Trabeculoplasty for Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG)" and "Iridotomy / Iridoplasty for Angle-Closure Glaucoma (CAG)" as worded in the US manual | Say Smart 532 treats or cures glaucoma, or list glaucoma as an indication in international copy (the international A.3.1 list omits it; A.2.1 names the procedures only as "ophthalmic procedures") |
| Retinopathy of prematurity | Respect the contraindication: "In infants with Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP), treatment with the Smart532 is contraindicated" (international manual) | Mention infants or ROP as a use of Smart 532, or reuse ROP wording from the Vision One section |
| Other specialties | Keep ear, nose and throat, dermatologic and dentistry uses out of Eye Care copy | Add them to retina or glaucoma pieces |
| Who uses it | "intended to be used only by physicians trained in the use of these instruments" | Suggest use by non-physicians |
| Safety | Pair benefit claims with the relevant risk language (increased macular edema, bleeding in areas of neovascularization) | Describe photocoagulation as free of risk, or omit "Never treat close to the fovea when using the CW mode" in CW-mode instructions |
| Prescription status | Carry "Rx Only – Caution: Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician." where the channel needs it | Call it over the counter or unrestricted |
| Regulatory status | Only cite a clearance if a source states it. The Array LaserLink manual lists "Smart532 Laser System (K151109)" under "*Premarket FDA Clearance numbers" | Say "FDA-approved"; none of the sources uses that word |
| SmartPulse mode | Describe it as the sub-threshold mode the manual describes ("the intended effect is sub-threshold") | Say it is suitable for panretinal photocoagulation or retinopexy: the international manual says it "is not recommended for ophthalmic procedures that require intense photocoagulation (e.g., for pan-retinal photocoagulation or retinopexy)" (A.2.5, p.A-4) |

## Array LaserLink

Array LaserLink is a **delivery device** (a slit-lamp pattern scanner), not a laser. Source: UM-1098750 Rev. F, (c) 2018-2021.

Introduction (p.7):

> The Array LaserLink delivery device adapts to a Slit Lamp for use as a therapeutic laser delivery devices, utilizing a scanning pattern generator to deliver predefined patterns of treatment. It is intended for use by an Ophthalmologist within a clinic or surgeon's office environment on patients regardless of age, gender, weight, or race. As the Lumenis Array LaserLink is for professional use only the device qualifies for exemption per 21 CFR 801 Subpart D.
>
> CAUTION – Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.

Indications for Use (p.72):

> Device Name: Lumenis® Array™ LaserLink™
>
> The Lumenis® Array™ LaserLink™ device is a laser system accessory intended for use in the treatment of ocular pathology.
> - For the posterior segment, the Lumenis® Array™ LaserLink™ device is indicated for use in retinal photocoagulation and panretinal photocoagulation of vascular and structural abnormalities of the retina and choroid including:
>   - Proliferative and severe and very severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy
>   - Macular edema associated with proliferative or non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy
>   - Choroidal neovascularization
>   - Retinal neovascularization associated with retinal occlusive disease (Branch retinal vein occlusion; Central retinal vein occlusion)
>   - Macular edema associated with Branch retinal vein occlusion
>   - Retinal tears and detachments
> - And anterior segments as follows:
>   - Iridotomy in closed angle glaucoma
>   - Trabeculoplasty in open angle glaucoma

Compatible lasers and clearance numbers (pp.10 and 72):

> The Array LaserLink delivery device must be installed by a Lumenis-certified technician, and is only compatible with specific Lumenis laser systems:
> Vision One™ Laser System (K111213*)
> Smart532™ Laser System (K151109*)
> *Premarket FDA Clearance numbers

Table 1, Pattern Indications for Use (p.73) restricts which patterns go with which condition. Examples: panretinal photocoagulation for proliferative diabetic retinopathy uses "All patterns except circle/half circle arc"; "Focal Photocoagulation" for macular edema and "Focal Laser" for choroidal neovascularization use "Single Spot (no pattern)"; "Primary Open-Angle Trabeculoplasty" and "Iridotomy" use "Single Spot (no pattern)".

Contraindications in Ophthalmology (p.74):

> Laser surgery with the Array LaserLink attached to a laser system is contraindicated when an appropriate procedure cannot be performed safely. This occurs when target tissue cannot be visualized properly. Corneal opacities, cataract formation and vitreous hemorrhage can all interfere with the laser surgeon's view of appropriate target structures and important tissue structure adjacent to the target tissue might be photocoagulated inadvertently. Under such circumstances treatment should be delayed until the ocular media problem resolves or is corrected.
>
> In treating eyes with no pigmentation, such as with albinism, laser photocoagulation should not be used as photocoagulation requires pigment in the retinal pigment epithelial cells for adequate absorption of laser energy.

Potential side effects or complications (p.75) lists, for retinal photocoagulation, "permanent central scotoma from inadvertent foveal burns", "vitreous hemorrhage if neovascularization is present", "rupture of Bruch's membrane from application of high fluence burns", among others; for laser iridotomy "acute increase in IOP" and "cataract"; for laser trabeculoplasty "increased interocular pressure" and "progression of visual field loss".

Brochure PB-2005744 Rev. B, p.3 of 6 (risk disclaimer, verbatim):

> Risks and warnings: Array is intended solely for use by trained physicians. It is contraindicated for eyes with severe media opacities. Risks include increased macular edema and bleeding in areas of neovascularization. Refer to the operator manual for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks.

### Do's and don'ts (Array LaserLink)

| Topic | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | "a laser system accessory" and "delivery device" that "adapts to a Slit Lamp" and delivers "predefined patterns" | Call it a laser, or say it works on any laser or any slit lamp: the manual says it is "only compatible with specific Lumenis laser systems" |
| Indications | Use the Indications for Use list (p.72) as written: retinal and panretinal photocoagulation for the listed retinal conditions; iridotomy in closed angle glaucoma; trabeculoplasty in open angle glaucoma | Add conditions (for example age-related macular degeneration by name, central serous chorioretinopathy) that the list does not name |
| Patterns | State that patterns are tied to the indication (Table 1) | Say every pattern can be used for every procedure; focal, choroidal neovascularization, trabeculoplasty and iridotomy are single spot only |
| Compatibility | Name Vision One and Smart 532 as the manual does | Name Novus Spectra without confirming (the 2017 brochure does; the Rev. F manual does not; see Open question 5) |
| Users | "intended for use by an Ophthalmologist" and "trained physicians" | Position it for use by non-physicians |
| Regulatory status | Clearance numbers only for the compatible lasers, as the manual states them ("Premarket FDA Clearance numbers") | Say the Array LaserLink itself is "FDA-approved" or give it a clearance number; the manual gives none for the Array |
| Prescription status | Carry "Federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician." | Omit it from US-facing pieces where the channel requires it |
| Risks | Carry the brochure risk line, or a shorter form pointing to the operator manual | Show benefits (time savings, patient comfort) without the risk statement |

## Vision One

Source: UM-1053000 Rev. H, effective April 2019, (c) Lumenis, Inc.

Indications for Use (p.78):

> The use of a laser instrument for an application is at the physician's discretion except in cases where the indication has been contraindicated.
>
> The Vision One is intended for use in the treatment of ocular pathology. The Vision One green, yellow, and red wavelengths are indicated for use in photocoagulation of both anterior and posterior segments including:
> - Retinal photocoagulation, panretinal photocoagulation, and intravitreal endophotocoagulation of vascular and structural abnormalities of the retina and choroid including:
>   - Proliferative and nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy
>   - Choroidal neovascularization
>   - Branch retinal vein occlusion
>   - Retinal tears and detachments
>   - Retinopathy of prematurity
> - Iridotomy, iridectomy, and trabeculoplasty in angle closure glaucoma and open angle glaucoma

Delivery-device indications (p.84):

> The Laser Indirect Ophthalmoscope is indicated for use in the following ophthalmic treatments:
> - diabetic retinopathy (pan-retinal photocoagulation)
> - retinopexy
> - segmental peripheral photocoagulation
> - segmental photocoagulation
> - cloudy vitreous cavities
> - pediatric retinal repairs (under general anesthesia)

> The Lumenis endoprobe, when used to deliver laser energy, is an effective tool for both anterior and posterior segment photocoagulation. Anterior segment treatment of the iris or ciliary body is effective in the surgical management of glaucoma.

Contraindications in Ophthalmology (p.81):

> Laser surgery with the Vision One laser is contraindicated when an appropriate procedure cannot be performed safely. This occurs when tissue targets can not be visualized properly. [...] Corneal opacities, cataract formation and vitreous hemorrhage can all interfere with the laser surgeon's view of appropriate target structures. Treatment should be delayed until the ocular media problem resolves or is corrected. If it is not possible to delay treatment or correct the ocular media problem, an alternative form of therapy should be implemented, if available, and medically indicated.

Warnings in Ophthalmology (p.82):

> Only appropriate tissue targets should be treated during photocoagulation.
> - Major retinal vessels should not be photocoagulated because vitreous hemorrhage and serious visual loss could occur.
> - The optic nerve should not be photocoagulated because it can cause severe and permanent visual loss.
> - The macula should not be photocoagulated except in procedures specifically designed for macular problems because inadvertent macular photocoagulation can cause severe and permanent visual loss.
> - Excessively high power settings should not be used because they can cause unnecessary tissue damage.

Required disclaimers (p.71 and label list p.75):

> CAUTION - US law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.
>
> CAUTION - Lumenis medical lasers and laser delivery devices are intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments.
>
> CE-labeled devices comply with all appropriate performance standards as specified in Annex II of the European Medical Device Directive MDD 93/42/EEC.
>
> FDA sales restriction label: CAUTION: FEDERAL LAW (U.S.A.) RESTRICTS THIS DEVICE TO SALE BY OR ON ORDER OF A [PHYSICIAN]

(The label wording is split across lines in the extracted text; the last word is reconstructed from the Novus Spectra label, which reads "...OR ON ORDER OF A PHYSICIAN.")

### Do's and don'ts (Vision One)

| Topic | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Wavelengths | "green, yellow, and red wavelengths" are the indicated wavelengths | Quote a wavelength set the system is not configured with, or suggest the three wavelengths are interchangeable for every condition |
| Indications | Use the p.78 list as written, including anterior and posterior segment photocoagulation | Add conditions that are not on the list (for example central serous chorioretinopathy or age-related macular degeneration by name) |
| Retinopathy of prematurity | If ROP is mentioned, quote the list entry and the physician-discretion sentence, and route to legal review | Carry the ROP entry to Smart 532 (whose international manual contraindicates ROP), Array LaserLink or SmartLIO copy |
| Glaucoma | "Iridotomy, iridectomy, and trabeculoplasty in angle closure glaucoma and open angle glaucoma" | Say it treats or cures glaucoma |
| Physician discretion | Keep "at the physician's discretion except in cases where the indication has been contraindicated" with indication copy | Imply an unrestricted application |
| Risks | Pair benefits with the vessel, optic nerve and macula warnings | Describe macular photocoagulation as routine |
| Prescription status | "US law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician." | Omit for US audiences |
| Regulatory status | The Array LaserLink manual lists "Vision One™ Laser System (K111213)" as a premarket FDA clearance number | Say "FDA-approved", or cite a clearance number from any other source |

## Novus Spectra

Source: UM-10039390 Rev. F, effective April 2019. This manual is for the "Novus Spectra Family of Lasers, Solid-State 532-nm Green Laser" and also contains non-ophthalmic (otologic) indications at p.95. Those are **not reproduced here and not for use in retina or glaucoma copy.**

Posterior segment, Indications (p.84):

> The laser is indicated for posterior segment procedures involving the retina.
>
> The Conditions of the retina for which the laser is useful include diabetic retinopathy, retinal detachments, and age-related macular degeneration, among others.

Contraindications, posterior segment (p.84):

> Contraindications for using the laser include opacities in the cornea or lens, or blood in the vitreous humor that may interfere with the delivery of laser energy to the desired structure.

The same page lists additional contraindications for panretinal photocoagulation, beginning "Grade IV or greater glial proliferation exists throughout the posterior pole of the eye."

Complications (p.86):

> The most common complication of panretinal photocoagulation is increased macular edema secondary to panretinal photocoagulation, usually with a concurrent decrease in visual acuity.

Anterior segment, Indications and uses (p.90):

> The laser is indicated to ablate tissues of the iris or trabeculum.
>
> Other uses of the laser in ophthalmology involve the anterior segment of the eye. The two most common procedures are performed to control glaucoma. These procedures are the peripheral iridotomy for angle-closure glaucoma and trabeculoplasty for open-angle glaucoma.
>
> Laser trabeculoplasty is not a cure for glaucoma, but another means by which operative surgery may be postponed in many patients.

Contraindications, anterior segment (p.90) begin "The following conditions are contraindicated to laser trabeculoplasty:" and list, among others, "Neovascular glaucoma" and "Glaucoma secondary to active uveitis".

Warnings, anterior segment (p.91):

> Intraocular pressure should be closely monitored following laser iridotomy or trabeculoplasty.
>
> Medical therapy should be continued following laser trabeculoplasty.

Laser indirect ophthalmoscope with the Novus Spectra (p.104):

> The indirect ophthalmoscope is indicated for the delivery of laser energy in eyes harboring peripheral retinal pathology such as retinal breaks, lattice degeneration, or peripheral neovascularization.
>
> The LIO should not be used in or near the macula, or in other areas of the eye where accuracy more precise than 300 um is required. The instabilities inherent in the aiming of the LIO preclude its use in these areas.

Slit lamp delivery systems and LaserLinks with the Novus Spectra (p.108):

> Lumenis slit lamp attachments and integrated slit lamps are indicated for the delivery of laser energy in posterior chamber procedures involving the retina.

Required disclaimers (p.16):

> CAUTION - US law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician.
>
> CAUTION - Lumenis medical lasers and laser delivery systems are intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments.
>
> CE-labeled devices comply with all appropriate performance standards as specified in Annex II of the European Medical Device Directive MDD 93/42/EEC.

Brochure PB-1036850 Rev. D, p.6 of 8 (risk disclaimer, verbatim):

> Novus Spectra is intended solely for use by trained physicians. It is contraindicated for eyes with severe media opacities. Risks include increased macular edema and bleeding in areas of neovascularization. Refer to the operator manual for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks.

### Do's and don'ts (Novus Spectra)

| Topic | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Indications | "posterior segment procedures involving the retina" and "to ablate tissues of the iris or trabeculum" | Use the otologic indications in eye-care copy, or add retinal conditions the manual does not mention |
| Retinal conditions | The manual's examples: diabetic retinopathy, retinal detachments and age-related macular degeneration "among others" | Present the example list as a complete indication list (it says "among others"; it is a usefulness statement, not a formal indication list) |
| Glaucoma | Say the manual describes iridotomy for angle-closure glaucoma and trabeculoplasty for open-angle glaucoma as "the two most common procedures" and that trabeculoplasty is "not a cure for glaucoma" | Call laser trabeculoplasty a cure or a permanent solution |
| LIO use | Describe LIO delivery as for "peripheral retinal pathology" | Describe LIO use in or near the macula (the manual states it "should not be used in or near the macula") |
| Users | "intended solely for physicians trained in the use of these instruments" | Position it for non-physicians |
| Risks | Carry the brochure risk line or a pointer to the manual | Present benefits without risk language |
| Regulatory status | State a clearance only if a source quotes it; none of the Novus Spectra documents on file gives a clearance number | Say "FDA-approved" or "FDA-cleared" for Novus Spectra without a source |

## SmartLIO (Smart Laser Indirect Ophthalmoscope)

SmartLIO is a **delivery device**. Source: UM-2103030EN Rev. A, March 2017.

Introduction (p.7):

> The SmartLIO is intended for use by medical professionals trained in the use of ophthalmic laser equipment and procedures.
>
> The SmartLIO is indicated for use in photocoagulation procedures of ocular tissue in the treatment of diseases of the eye (see "Indications for Use" on page 18).

Indications for Use (p.18):

> The Lumenis Smart Laser Indirect Ophthalmoscope (LIO) is a battery-powered optical instrument intended for the viewing of the posterior segment of the eye and to deliver laser energy for photocoagulation procedures on the peripheral retina of the eye (in conjunction with the use of a hand-held condensing lens).
>
> The SmartLIO is indicated for use in the following photocoagulation procedures:
> - Panretinal photocoagulation;
> - Segmental peripheral photocoagulation;
> - Retinopexy; and,
> - Pediatric retinal repairs (under general anesthesia).
>
> The SmartLIO is intended to work in conjunction with the following Lumenis laser systems in ophthalmic photocoagulation procedures:

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Smart 532 indications differ by edition.** The US edition (UM-1151440ENBe Rev. A, 2023) lists choroidal neovascularization secondary to AMD, central serous chorioretinopathy, POAG trabeculoplasty and ACG iridotomy/iridoplasty, and also ENT, dermatologic and dentistry indications. The international edition (UM-1151441BeEN Rev. A, Nov 2025) lists only diabetic retinopathy, proliferative diabetic retinopathy, retinal tear and detachment, and macular edema or proliferative retinopathy with CRVO or BRVO under "Indications for Use", names trabeculoplasty, iridoplasty and iridotomy only as general intended uses, and contraindicates ROP. Confirm which wording governs each market and whether the US list is still current, since the 2025 document is newer.
2. **Smart 532 revision currency.** The US manual "Rev. A" (Feb 2023, catalog UM-1151440ENBe) is later by date than "Rev. H" (March 2019, catalog UM-1151440EN). It looks like a re-issued series that restarted at A after the Lumenis Be, Inc. change, but nothing on file says so. Confirm which is current for the US, and confirm the index revision flags (Rev. A superseded by Rev. H; international files flagged "Rev E") are wrong.
3. **Retinopathy of prematurity.** The Vision One manual lists "Retinopathy of prematurity" under indications for green, yellow and red wavelengths; the Smart 532 international manual says ROP treatment is contraindicated at 532 nm; the archived 2017 Vision One brochure (PB-2005743 Rev B) says green and yellow are "not recommended for treatment of retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)". Confirm the intended Vision One wording, and that ROP stays out of Smart 532 copy.
4. **SmartLIO US status.** The 2017 Smart 532 brochure and the archived Retina brochure mark the Smart LIO "Pending US market clearance". The SmartLIO manual (March 2017) carries US Rx wording but no clearance number. The Smart 532 international manual (Nov 2025) calls the Smart LIO an accessory of the Smart532. Confirm current US clearance status before any US claim.
5. **Array LaserLink compatibility.** The Rev. F manual lists Vision One and Smart532 only; the 2017 brochure PB-2005744 Rev B lists Vision One and Novus Spectra, and the SmartLIO and Novus Spectra manuals discuss LaserLinks generally. Confirm whether Array works with Novus Spectra. The Rev. F title page shows "(c) 2018-2021" with no effective date, so its age cannot be established beyond that.
6. **EU wording.** The Novus Spectra, Vision One, SmartLIO and LIO Keeler documents cite the Medical Device Directive MDD 93/42/EEC; no EU MDR (2017/745) wording was found in the Smart 532 international manual text. Confirm the current EU status statement before EU copy is drafted.
7. **Novus Spectra and LIO Keeler age.** The Novus Spectra manual (Rev. F, 2019) and brochure (Rev. D, 2010) and the LIO Keeler manual (2006) are the only sources for those products. Confirm they are still current, particularly because the SmartLIO manual (2017) says SmartLIO supports Novus Spectra and neither Novus document mentions SmartLIO.
8. **Novus Spectra LIO contraindication.** "The LIO should not be used in or near the macula" is in the Novus Spectra manual only. Confirm whether it applies to SmartLIO, which has no contraindications section on file, before reusing it for SmartLIO.
9. **Selecta family.** No manual, IFU or indication text was found for Selecta Duet, Trio or the Selecta platform. Provide the controlled documents so a section can be written.
10. **What was and was not checked.** Every quote above was copied from the extracted text of the cited page. Page numbers are the printed page labels, which matched the PDF page order in each file checked (appendix pages in the Smart 532 manuals use the A-n labels). Not checked: the printed PDFs themselves (only extracted text was read), the full text of the Chinese manual, the translated Smart 532 manuals beyond their title pages, and the adverse-effect and precaution passages not quoted here.
