# Optima IPL and M22 regulatory language (legacy platform)

> **LEGACY PLATFORM. Read this first.**
>
> - Optima IPL / M22 (eye care) and Stellar M22 (aesthetics) is a **legacy platform**. Optima IPL is the predecessor of OptiLIGHT.
> - **OptiLIGHT is the current dry eye product.** Its indications, contraindications and wording are in [optilight.md](optilight.md).
> - **Nothing on this page may be used for OptiLIGHT claims**, and nothing from the OptiLIGHT page may be used for Optima IPL. The two have different indications, markets and source documents. Do not copy a dry eye indication, a skin type range, a contraindication or a statistic from here into OptiLIGHT copy.
> - This page exists so that legacy materials still in circulation can be checked, not so that new copy can be written from them.

The platform is sold by two divisions. **Eye care (Optima IPL / M22 with the Universal IPL module, dry eye) and aesthetics (Stellar M22, skin) are kept in separate sections below. Never merge them.** The eye-care and aesthetic indications are different, and the dry eye indication is not available in the United States.

Quotes are taken from the extracted text of the source PDFs, with line breaks joined. Spelling and punctuation are the source's own, including source typos, which are marked [sic].

# Part 1. Eye care: Optima IPL / M22 with the Universal IPL module (dry eye)

## Indication for use (verbatim)

**EU and other markets where approved, UM-1034720 Rev A, section A.3.1 "Intended use and Indications", p.6:**

> The M22-IPL system is indicated for the treatment of Evaporative Dry Eye Disease, also known as dry eye syndrome or lipid tear deficiency, due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction
>
> This indication is intended for Fitzpatrick skin types I-V (note: for Fitzpatrick skin type V please refer to table 1 and 2)
>
> This indication has not been cleared in the United States by the FDA

The addendum defines "M22-IPL system" as "the M22 system with the Universal IPL module" (p.3). The referenced tables 1 and 2 are not in the extracted text. Scope statement, same addendum, p.3:

> This addendum for the M22 for the Dry Eye indication is only relevant where this indication was approved by the Regulatory Authorities. This indication has not been cleared in the United States by the FDA.

**United States:** no US dry eye indication for this platform appears in any source. The sources say the opposite (quotes in the next section). Do not write a US dry eye indication for Optima IPL.

Treatment of the signs and symptoms of Dry Eye Disease (DED) due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction, also known as evaporative dry eye or lipid deficiency dry eye has not been authorized for use in the United States by the FDA.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

**UM-1034720 Rev A, section A.3.2, pp.6-7:**

> Patients who have had prior problems with IPL treatment should be carefully screened before treatment.
>
> Treatment should not be attempted on patients with the following conditions in the treatment area (non-exhaustive list):
> - Active infections
> - Dysplastic nevi
> - Tattoos
> - Significant concurrent skin conditions or any inflammatory skin conditions.
> - Active cold sores, open lacerations or abrasions.
> - Chronic or cutaneous viral, fungal or bacterial diseases.
> - Exposure to sun or artificial tanning during 3-4 weeks prior to treatment.
> - Treatment should not be attempted on patients with a history or concurrent condition of skin cancer or pre-cancerous lesions at the treatment areas.

**PB-00024560 Rev A (2021), p.6, "Risks and warning (non-inclusive list)", newest brochure:**

> Lumenis energy based treatments are not suitable for everyone and, like every treatment, can carry some risks. Treatment with Stellar M22 Optima IPL is contra-indicated for patients with the following conditions in the treatment area: active infections, dysplastic nevi, open lacerations, abrasions, and prolonged sun-exposure. Caution is advised for patients with photosensitivity, immune/coagulation/hormonal disorders, or a history of Herpes simplex at or near the treatment area. Side effects include (but are not limited to) discomfort, excessive edema/erythema, bruising, burns, scarring, and changes in pigmentation. Please refer to the operator manual for a complete list of intended uses, contraindications and risks.

PB-2012273 Rev A (2019, p.1) has the same text with "M22 Optima IPL" in place of "Stellar M22 Optima IPL". PB-2005802 Rev C (2018, p.1) differs:

> Optima IPL is contraindicated for patients with active infections, dysplatic [sic] nevi, or tattoos in the treatment area. Optima IPL can cause epidermal injury, including redness, swelling, scarring, change of pigmentation, blisters, break in skin integrity, and sloughing. Risk of epidermal injury increases with greater fluence, shorter delays, shorter pulse durations, darker skin pigmentation, thin skin, and exposure to the sun within one month following treatment. Refer to the operator manual for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks.

The 2016 patient pieces (PB-2006590 Rev A, PB-2006589 Rev A and PB-2006593 Rev A) carry the longest brochure-style list:

> Treatment with M22 Optima IPL is contraindicated for patients with the following conditions in the treatment area: active infections, dysplastic nevi, tattoos, significant concurrent skin inflammation (excluding rosacea or acne), active cold sores, open lacerations, abrasions, and prolonged sun-exposure. Caution is advised for patients with a compromised immune system, coagulation disorders, photosensitivity, hormonal disorders, or a history of Herpes simplex near the treatment area. Patients eyes must be occluded with eye shields during the treatment.

(PB-2006593 Rev A, the referral card, prints the same text.) The 2021 and 2019 brochure lists omit tattoos; the manual addendum, the 2018 brochure and the 2016 pieces include it. See Open questions.

## Precautions (verbatim)

**UM-1034720 Rev A, section A.3.3, p.7:**

> Patients with a medical history or presence of the following should be treated with caution and per the physician's discretion:
> - Bleeding coagulopathies.
> - Keloid scarring.
> - Herpes simplex; treatment can trigger a herpes outbreak; prophylactic antiviral therapy may be prescribed at the physician's discretion.
> - Systemic Lupus Erythematosus or Porphyria.
> - Immunosuppressive diseases, including AIDS and HIV infection.
> - Uncontrolled systemic diseases such as diabetes, epilepsy or congestive heart disease.
> - Photosensitivity in general, or any sensitivity to the sun that causes a rash or an allergic reaction.
> - Hormonal disorders, such as polycystic ovary syndrome, unless under control.
> - Gold therapy.
> - Radiation therapy.
> - Prior use of collagen, fat injections or other methods of skin augmentation.
>
> Patients who use any of the following should be treated with caution and per the physician's discretion:
> - Anticoagulants; avoid usage of anticoagulants prior to treatment, at the physician's discretion.
> - Immunosuppressive medications.
> - Herbal supplements, perfumes or cosmetics that may affect sensitivity to light.
> - Oral Isotretinoin (such as Accutane).

## Warnings (verbatim)

**UM-1034720 Rev A, section A.3.4, pp.8-9:**

> Patients with the following conditions should be treated with caution and per the physician's discretion:
> - Damage to natural skin texture and/or very dry skin.
> - Highly vasculated area in the lesion's immediate proximity.
> - Treatment within the orbital rim: patient should wear intraocular shields to protect the eyes from direct or indirect IPL exposure.
>
> IPL can cause epidermal injury. The risk increases with greater fluence and skin pigmentation:
> - Darker skin types and individuals with a non-recent suntan are at a higher risk for pigmentary changes in the treatment area.
> - Sun exposure to the treatment area immediately after treatment and for one month following treatment may also increase the risk of pigmentary changes in the treatment area. Patients should be instructed to use a broad spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 at least) on a daily basis, and repeat its application every two hours. Direct sun exposure should be avoided.
> - Perform test spots on patients and assess skin response before performing a full treatment. Side effects may not develop until several days following exposure particularly in darker skin types. Always allow adequate time between test spot and actual treatment.
>
> All persons in the treatment room, including the patient, the operator and any observers must wear appropriate eye protection whenever the main power is on.
> - IPL can cause permanent damage to pigmented structures of the eyes, if eyes are not protected with fully occlusive eye shields during treatment. IPL treatment when eyes are not fully protected can cause iritis, iris atrophy, trans-illumination defects, pupillary defects and anterior uveitis. These may results [sic] in permanent damage to vision, photophobia, and pain.
> - Contact lenses should not be worn during IPL treatment.
>
> Note: Lumenis has no clinical information concerning the safety of light treatment on pregnant or nursing women.

## Treatment area, expectations and side effects (verbatim)

**UM-1034720 Rev A, section A.4.1, p.9:**

> IPL treatment of DED due to MGD is performed from tragus to tragus including the cheeks, the nose, and around the periorbital area.

**Section A.4.2 "Counseling", pp.9-10:**

> - There may be some discomfort or pain associated with IPL treatment (see Section A.4.4).
> - There may be some discomfort or pain associated with manual expression of the meibomian glands (see Section A.11.1).
> - Transient erythema/edema may appear immediately following treatment.
> - Acceptable results will likely take a number of treatments, usually three to four treatments, performed at 2-4 weeks intervals. The entire program should be planned at the outset to promote compliance and realistic expectations.
> - There is a small risk of adverse reactions such as changes in the texture and pigmentation of the skin. These are usually transient and rare (see Section A.4.5).
> - IPL treatment may cause hair loss, including eyebrows and eyelashes.

**Section A.4.5.10 "Ocular complications", p.12:**

> Periorbital IPL treatment may permanently affect pigmented ocular structures if eyes are not protected with fully occlusive eye shields during the treatment. Ocular complications include pupillary defects, iritis, anterior uveitis, posterior synechiae, and iris trans-illumination defects. These complications can lead to pain, photophobia and, in extreme cases, permanent damage to vision. Application of IPL on the eyelashes may lead to permanent loss of eyelashes. Therefore, do not touch any part of the eyelashes with the IPL lightguide.

Other listed side effects (section A.4.5, pp.11-12) include discomfort, "A crust or blister may form, which may take from five to ten days to heal", change of pigmentation, scarring, swelling ("Swelling usually subsides within hours to as much as seven days"), fragile skin, bruising, burns and pruritus. See the addendum for the full text.

**Device label text, PB-00024560 Rev A (label LB-20036100_B, newest) p.6:**

> Risk GROUP 3 Wavelength: 400-1200 nm Max Fluence: 56 J/cm² Pulse Duration: 4-20 ms WARNING IR emitted from this device may cause eye injury Avoid eye exposure CAUTION The light emitted may be harmful to the eyes Do not stare at the light source IEC60601-2-57 Ed 1 LB-20036100_B

The earlier label text in PB-2012273 Rev A (LB-20036100_A) reads "WARNING The light emitted may result in eye injury Do not look at the light source CAUTION IR emitted from this device may cause eye irritation Do not stare at the light source". Use the _B text.

## What this means for copy (eye care)

- **Legacy, not OptiLIGHT.** Do not use this page, or Optima IPL claims, for OptiLIGHT. OptiLIGHT's indications are on [optilight.md](optilight.md).
- **No US dry eye claim.** Every eye-care source from 2018 to 2021 says the dry eye indication has not been cleared or authorized in the US. Copy for the US cannot present this platform as indicated for dry eye.
- **Keep the market and the condition straight.** Where approved, the indication is evaporative dry eye disease due to MGD, Fitzpatrick skin types I-V. It is not "dry eye" in general, and the sources give no indication for rosacea of the eyelids or for "periocular inflammation" as a stand-alone claim for the Universal IPL module in eye care.
- **"FDA cleared" is about the platform, not dry eye.** The brochures say "FDA cleared" for M22 and, in the same piece, say the dry eye indication is not cleared or authorized in the US. Never put the two together.
- **Eyes must be shielded.** Treatment is "from tragus to tragus including the cheeks, the nose, and around the periorbital area" with "fully occlusive eye shields".
- **Keep the side-effect picture.** The addendum lists blisters, swelling for up to seven days, hair loss including eyebrows and eyelashes, and ocular complications.

# Part 2. Aesthetics: Stellar M22

## Indications for use (verbatim)

All from UM-1024721 Rev J (March 2019). Chapter 1 and the clinical appendices state the indications in different detail. They are given as printed.

**Section 1.1 "Introduction and Intended Use", p.1-1:**

> The Lumenis M22 system has connection capability with the following available treatment modules, for multi-application treatment options. All modules are designed for aesthetic and dermatological skin procedure application, as follows:
> - Intense Pulsed Light (IPL) with a spectrum of 400-1200nm (with different filters) is indicated for:
>   - Benign epidermal lesions, including dyschromia, hyperpigmentation, melisma [sic] and ephelides (freckles).
>   - Cutaneous lesions, including warts, scars and striae.
>   - Benign cutaneous vascular lesions including port wine stains, hemangiomas, facial, truncal and leg telangiectasias, erythema of rosacea, angiomas and spider angiomas, poikiloderma of Civatte, leg veins and venous malformations.
>   - Removal of unwanted hair to effect stable long term, or permanent hair reduction in skin types I-V through selective targeting of melanin in hair follicles.
>   - Mild to moderate inflammatory acne (acne vulgaris).
> - Multi-Spot Nd:YAG Laser, with a wavelength of 1064 nm is indicated for:
>   - The coagulation and hemostasis of vascular lesions and soft tissue, including the treatment and clearance of superficial and deep telangiectasias (venulectasias) and reticular veins (0.1 - 4.0 mm diameter) of the leg.
>   - The removal of unwanted hair from all skin types, and to effect stable long term, or permanent hair reduction in skin types I-V through selective targeting of melanin in hair follicles.
>   - The non-ablative treatment of facial wrinkles.
> - ResurFX module and handpiece, with wavelength of 1565 nm, is indicated for use in dermatological procedures requiring skin resurfacing and coagulation of soft tissue.
> - Q-Switched Nd:YAG Laser, with a wavelength of 1064 nm, is indicated for:
>   - Removal of dark tattoos.
>   - Treatment of pigmented lesions.
>
> Note: Permanent hair reduction is defined as the long-term, stable reduction in the number of hairs regrowing when measured at 6, 9 and 12 months after the completion of a treatment regimen.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

**Appendix A, IPL Skin Treatments, section A.3.2, pp.A-1 to A-2:**

> Patients who have had prior problems with IPL treatment should be carefully screened before treatment. Treatment should not be attempted on patients with the following conditions in the treatment area:
> - Active infections
> - Dysplastic nevi
> - Tattoos
> - Significant concurrent skin conditions or any inflammatory skin conditions.
> - Active cold sores, open lacerations or abrasions.
> - Chronic or cutaneous viral, fungal or bacterial diseases.
> - Exposure to sun or artificial tanning during 3-4 weeks prior to treatment.
>
> Treatment should not be attempted on patients with a history or concurrent condition of skin cancer or pre-cancerous lesions at the treatment areas.

Other appendices repeat this list and add or change lines. The lines that differ, quoted:

- Appendix B, vascular lesions, p.B-2: "With IPL, treatment should not be attempted on patients with Goldman-Fitzpatrick Skin Type VI."
- Appendices E (p.E-2) and F (p.F-2), ResurFX: "Exposure to sun in the 4-6 weeks pre-op, or artificial tanning in the past 2-3 weeks pre-op, remaining suntan, sunburn or artificially toned skin." and "Permanent dermal implants" (and "Tattoos"; "Treatment should not be attempted on patients with a history of skin cancer or pre-cancerous lesions on the treatment area.").
- Appendix G, Q-Switched, pp.G-1 to G-2: "Tattoo younger than six months or cosmetic tattoo.", "Skin type which is darker than the tattoo or the pigmented lesion (mostly VI)." and "Tattoo with iron- or titanium-oxide (if the patient knows the pigment composition)."

Use the manual's appendix for the module in question for the complete list.

## Precautions and warnings (verbatim)

The precaution lists in Appendices A to D and G match the eye-care list quoted in Part 1 (section A.3.3 of the manual, p.A-2). The ResurFX appendices (E.3.3, F.3.3) use their own "Cautions" list. Read the appendix for the module.

**Appendix A, section A.3.4 "Warnings", p.A-3:**

> The IPL can cause epidermal injury. The risk increases with greater fluence and skin pigmentation:
> - Darker skin types and individuals with a remaining suntan are at a higher risk for pigmentary changes in the treatment area. These patients should be treated with caution following strict test-spot protocol.
> - Sun exposure to the treatment area immediately after treatment and for one month following treatment may also increase the risk of pigmentary changes in the treatment area. Patients should be instructed to use a broad spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 at least) on a daily basis, and repeat its application every two hours. Direct sun exposure should be avoided.
> - The light emitted by the module is capable of causing serious eye damage or blindness. All persons in the treatment room, including the patient, the operator and any observers must wear appropriate eye protection whenever the main power is on.
> - Perform test spots on patients and assess skin response before performing a full treatment. Side effects may not develop until several days following exposure. For skin type V, wait at least 48-72 hours after test spots to observe tissue reaction. Always allow adequate time between test spot and actual treatment.
>
> Note: Lumenis has no clinical information concerning the safety of light treatment on pregnant or nursing women.

**Appendix A, section A.4.1 "General", p.A-4 (what the treatment is not):**

> IPL Skin Treatments are not intended to be a substitute for surgical facelift, ablative skin resurfacing, or procedures that treat deep wrinkles and sagging skin. It is intended for patients desiring evenness of color in their complexions, or those showing signs of sun damage and photo-aging.
>
> IPL Acne Treatment is intended for individuals with mild to moderate cases of inflammatory acne. The treatment will not substitute prescription drugs or surgical treatments for comedones, large cysts and abscesses in severe cases of acne.
>
> IPL Skin Treatments have great appeal to individuals with active lifestyles who cannot set aside healing time, as required with ablative procedures or surgery. The procedure requires no "downtime", i.e., patients can resume all activities immediately after treatment, except for sun exposure.

## What this means for copy (aesthetics)

- **Legacy, not OptiLIGHT or OptiLIFT.** Stellar M22 aesthetic copy does not carry over to Lumenis' current Vision products.
- **Name the module.** The indications are per module (IPL, Multi-Spot Nd:YAG, ResurFX, Q-Switched Nd:YAG). Do not describe a claim from one module as a claim for the system.
- **ResurFX depends on the market.** The US text lists coagulation of soft tissue and skin resurfacing. The non-US text adds dyschromia, acne and surgical scars, striae and periorbital wrinkles.
- **Hair: "reduction", and say how it is defined.** "Permanent" is attached to hair reduction in skin types I-V, with the 6, 9 and 12 month definition.
- **Tattoos: dark tattoos only.** The tattoo or lesion must be darker than the skin.
- **Eye protection and Rx.** Keep the eye-damage warning and the "Rx ONLY" or US federal law caution wherever the channel requires them.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Is Optima IPL really the predecessor of OptiLIGHT, and which pieces are still in market?** The "predecessor" statement comes from the instruction that scoped this page; no source document on file says so. The existing OptiLIGHT page treats a different, later set of documents. Confirm the relationship, whether any Optima IPL piece is still allowed in any market, and whether this page should stay in the public repository.
2. **No current eye-care manual or label for the Universal IPL module.** The eye-care sources are an EU addendum (UM-1034720 Rev A, April 2018) and archived brochures. The addendum predates the aesthetic manual Rev J (March 2019), which does not mention dry eye. Confirm that the addendum has not been revised, and which market list "approved by the Regulatory Authorities" refers to.
3. **Tattoos and the brochure contraindication list.** The addendum and the 2016 and 2018 pieces list tattoos as a contraindication; the 2019 and 2021 brochures do not. Confirm which is current, and whether brochure lists should be replaced by "refer to the operator manual".
4. **"Without any downtime", "long-lasting results", "displacing the need for medications", "treat the root cause".** These appear in the 2016-2021 pieces (the last two in the 2021 piece too). The addendum describes blisters, swelling up to seven days and hair loss, and gives no support for "long-lasting". Confirm whether these may be used at all.
5. **"FDA cleared" statement.** The 2018-2021 brochures state "M22 is FDA cleared" next to "The Dry eye indication has not been cleared in the United States". The 2016 footnote ties the only 510(k) number (K142860) to erythema of rosacea. Confirm the 510(k) numbers and cleared indications for the platform and the safe wording.
6. **Manual chapter 1 versus appendices.** Section 1.1 lists IPL indications that Appendix A does not ("warts", "ephelides (freckles)", "leg veins and venous malformations", "removal of unwanted hair") and Multi-Spot indications for hair and facial wrinkles, for which no clinical-guide appendix was found. Confirm which statement governs each market.
7. **ResurFX and striae in the US.** The Photofractional stretch mark trifold (PB-2020010 Rev B) sells IPL plus ResurFX for stretch marks. The US ResurFX indication (Appendix F) does not list striae; the IPL indication (Appendix A) does. Confirm whether the combination claim is supportable in the US.
8. **Skin types.** The addendum limits the dry eye indication to skin types I-V and refers to "table 1 and 2" for type V, which are not in the extracted text. The aesthetic ResurFX text gives skin types I-VI; the hair indication gives I-V for permanent reduction. Confirm how copy should state skin types.
9. **Universal IPL treatment head wavelength.** PB-1037980 Rev B (2009) gives the head's range as 515-1200 nm and filters from 515 nm; the manual and brochures give 400-1200 nm. Confirm which is current.
10. **Names.** PB-00024560 Rev A is filed as "Stellar M22 brochure" but its body text says "Optima IPL" and its table is headed "Stellar M22". Confirm the correct product names by market and year.
11. **Control ID detection.** The automatic index listed the 2019 brochure as LB-20036100 (that is the device label inside it; the document is PB-2012273 Rev A) and left the revision of PB-00024560 blank (footer says Rev A). The corrected values are used here.
12. **Not verified.** Page numbers for brochures refer to the extracted print or web file layout. Printed PDFs were not viewed; the tables 1 and 2 in the addendum and any images could not be checked.
