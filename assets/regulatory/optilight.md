# OptiLIGHT regulatory language

OptiLIGHT is an Eye Care product. This page covers the dry-eye indication only. The manuals also describe an "upgraded configuration" with aesthetic modules; those indications are in a separate, clearly labeled section at the end of the indication text and must never be used for dry-eye copy.

## Indication for use (verbatim)

### United States

> Improvement of signs of Dry Eye Disease (DED) due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD), also known as evaporative dry eye or lipid deficiency dry eye, in patients 22 years of age and older with moderate to severe signs and symptoms of DED due to MGD and with Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV. IPL is to be applied only to skin on the malar region of the face, from tragus to tragus including the nose (eyes should be fully covered by protective eyewear).
>
> IPL is intended to be applied as an adjunct to other modalities, such as meibomian gland expression, artificial tear lubricants and warm compresses.

Source: UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H, section 1.3 (p.1-5) and Appendix A, section A.3.1 (p.A-1), introduced as "The OptiLight system used in the OptiLight modality (the default configuration) is indicated for". The same text is in PB-00094760 Rev. A p.5, PB-00029930 Rev. C and PB-00030660 Rev. A slide 28.

### EU and other non-US markets

Newest manual, UM-0005500DIG Rev. F, section 1.3 (p.1-5) and Appendix A, section A.3 (p.A-1):

> Treatment of evaporative dry eye disease (DED), also known as dry eye syndrome or lipid tear deficiency, due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD).

Same manual, Appendix A, section A.5.1 (p.A-3), on skin types:

> IPL treatment of DED due to MGD is intended for patients suffering from DED due to MGD with Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV and V (the latter, in subjects of Indian descent who suffer from DED due to MGD, approved in EU only).

The Quick Reference Guide (PB-00094760 Rev. A, p.5) and the B2B brochure (PB-00029930 Rev. C) state the EU indication differently:

> **In EU:** Evaporative Dry Eye Disease (DED), also known as dry eye syndrome or lipid tear deficiency, due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD). This indication is intended for Fitzpatrick skin types I-V.

The same two documents close the indication with:

> The indications are only relevant where they were approved by the Regulatory Authorities.

PB-00029930 Rev. C also prints "INDICATIONS FOR USE MAY VARY ACCORDING TO LOCAL REGISTRATIONS AND APPROVALS."

The same EU indication sentence appears in the English-language manual UM-0005500DIGGR Rev. D (Lumenis GmbH, December 2025), section 1.3, checked for that section only. The EU wording differs from the US wording in verb ("Treatment of" vs. "Improvement of signs of"), in the absence of an age limit and a severity limit, and in skin-type scope. Keep the two apart. See Open questions 1 and 2.

### Upgraded configuration (aesthetic modules) - not for dry-eye copy

UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H, section 1.3 (p.1-5 to 1-6), continues: "In its upgraded configuration(s), a range of indications are added", then lists IPL, Multi-Spot Nd:YAG, ResurFX and Q-Switched Nd:YAG aesthetic and dermatologic indications. These belong to the Aesthetics business. They are outside this page; do not attach them to OptiLIGHT dry-eye copy, and do not use dry-eye indication wording for aesthetic modules. The aesthetic one-pagers PB-00054840 Rev. A and PB-00054850 Rev. A carry "Rx ONLY" and their own warnings and are not summarized here.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

### Title-card and brochure wording (PB-00094760 Rev. A p.5; PB-00029930 Rev. C)

> Treatment with OptiLIGHT is contraindicated for patients with the following conditions in the Treatment area: Ocular surgery or eyelid surgery or Neuro-paralysis within 6 months prior to the first treatment; Uncontrolled eye disorders affecting the ocular surface; Pre-cancerous lesions, skin cancer or pigmented lesions; Uncontrolled infections or uncontrolled immunosuppressive diseases; Recent Ocular infections; History of cold sores or rashes in the perioral area, including: Herpes simplex 1 & 2, Systemic Lupus erythematosus and porphyria; Use of photosensitive medication and/or herbs that may cause sensitivity within 3 months prior to the first IPL session; Recent radiation therapy to the head or neck or planned radiation therapy; Recent treatment with chemotherapeutic agent or planned chemotherapy; History of migraines, seizures or epilepsy. Patients eyes must be completely occluded during the treatment. Please refer to the operator manual for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks.

### Manual wording, DED-specific (UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H, section A.3.2, p.A-2; identical in UM-0005500DIG Rev. F, section A.9.1, pp.A-16 to A-17)

> The following contraindications are specific to the use of the OptiLight modality (the default configuration) for treatment of Dry Eye Disease due to MGD:
> - Ocular surgery or eyelid surgery, within 6 months prior to the first IPL session
> - Neuro-paralysis in the planned treatment area, within 6 months prior to the first IPL session
> - Uncontrolled eye disorders affecting the ocular surface, for example active allergies
> - Pre-cancerous lesions, skin cancer or pigmented lesions in the planned treatment area
> - Uncontrolled infections or uncontrolled immunosuppressive diseases
> - Ocular infections, within 6 months prior to the first IPL session
> - Prior history of cold sores or rashes in the perioral area or in the planned treatment area that could be stimulated by light at a wavelength of 560 nm to 1200 nm, including: Herpes simplex 1 & 2, Systemic Lupus erythematosus, and porphyria
> - Within 3 months prior to the first IPL session, use of photosensitive medication and/or herbs that may cause sensitivity to 560-1200 nm light exposure, including: Isotretinoin, Tetracycline, Doxycycline, and St. John's Wort
> - Radiation therapy to the head or neck, within 12 months prior to the first IPL session
> - Planned radiation therapy, within 8 weeks after the last IPL session
> - Treatment with chemotherapeutic agent, within 8 weeks prior to the first IPL session
> - Planned chemotherapy, within 8 weeks after the last IPL session
> - History of migraines, seizures or epilepsy

### Manual wording, general IPL (UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H, section 1.4.1, p.1-6; same in Rev. F, p.1-6)

> Tattoos
> Fitzpatrick Skin Type VI
> Exposure to sun or artificial tanning during 3-4 weeks prior to treatment
> Any remaining suntan, sunburn or artificial tanning products

Section 1.4 of both manuals also lists general contraindications "in the treatment area": active infections, dysplastic nevi, significant concurrent skin conditions or any inflammatory skin conditions, active cold sores, open lacerations or abrasions, chronic or cutaneous viral, fungal or bacterial diseases, and a history or concurrent condition of skin cancer or pre-cancerous lesions at the treatment areas.

### Quick Reference Guide clinical detail (PB-00094760 Rev. A p.4)

The guide lists "Absolute" contraindications (pregnancy/nursing; active HSV/cold sores; light-triggered migraines/epilepsy; light-sensitive autoimmune conditions such as lupus and porphyria; open wounds/compromised skin barrier; pre-cancerous or cancerous lesion in the treatment area; "Avoid treating over tattoos or permanent makeup") and "Temporary" ones (sun or artificial tanning in the 3-4 weeks prior; "PRK/LASIK: Wait 6 months post-surgery"; "Filler in treatment area should be treated with caution"; "Isotretinoin within 6-12 months of treatment"; radiation therapy to the head or neck "within 12 months prior to the first IPL session"). It instructs: "Discontinue photosensitive medications 3 months prior to treatment" and lists tetracycline, doxycycline, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, and isotretinoin ("Discontinue 6-12 months prior to treatment with OptiLIGHT/IPL"). It ends: "*Please refer to the user manual for a complete list of contraindications and risks." Where the guide and the manual differ, see Open question 5.

### Patient brochure wording (PB-00032060 Rev. C, patient risk information)

> Lumenis energy-based treatments are not suitable for everyone and, like every treatment, can carry some risks. Treatment with OptiLIGHT is contraindicated for patients with the following conditions in the treatment area: active infections, dysplastic nevi, tattoos, significant concurrent skin inflammation (excluding rosacea or acne), active cold sores, open lacerations, abrasions, and prolonged sun-exposure. Caution is advised for patients with a compromised immune system, coagulation disorders, photosensitivity, hormonal disorders, or a history of Herpes simplex near the treatment area. Patients' eyes must be occluded with eye shields during the treatment. Be sure to consult with your treatment provider to make sure that this treatment is the right choice for you.

## Side effects (verbatim)

> The following possible side effects can occur following IPL treatments: Pain/discomfort, damage to natural skin texture, change of pigmentation, scarring, excessive edema, fragile skin, bruising, burns, pruritus and xerosis. Please refer to the user manual or ask your doctor for a complete list of intended use, contraindications and risks.

Source: PB-00094760 Rev. A p.5 and PB-00029930 Rev. C (this form); the same list, in lower case, is in UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H, section A.5.4 (p.A-6).

EU manual, UM-0005500DIG Rev. F, section A.9.3 (p.A-19):

> Transient mild erythema, mild edema, and mild to moderate pain/discomfort are expected side effects.

> Complications specific to IPL treatment of DED due to MGD include loss of eyelashes, loss of hair in eyebrows, eye irritation, irritation of the periorbital skin, and ocular complications. [...] Ocular complications include pupillary defects, iritis, anterior uveitis, posterior synechiae, and iris trans-illumination defects. These complications can lead to ocular pain, photophobia, and, in extreme cases, permanent damage to vision.

(Brackets mark a footnote reference removed from the quote.) Rev. H, section A.5.2 (pp.A-5 to A-6) lists the patient information to give: "There may be some discomfort or pain associated with IPL treatment", "Transient erythema/edema may appear immediately following treatment", and that "Results should be expected after several treatments. The recommended schedule consists of four treatments, performed at 2 weeks intervals."

## What this means for copy

- **Keep "due to MGD."** OptiLIGHT's indication is dry eye disease due to MGD, not dry eye in general.
- **Keep the market straight.** US copy uses the US indication: adults 22 and older, moderate to severe signs and symptoms, Fitzpatrick I-IV, malar region only, as an adjunct to expression, artificial tears and warm compresses. EU copy uses the EU indication and must be checked against the market's registration. Never mix them, and never use the EU "Treatment of" wording in US copy.
- **"Improvement of signs."** The US indication covers improvement of signs of DED due to MGD. Symptom and relief claims need a cited study from the [claims library](../claims-reference/optilight.md). In the pivotal study reproduced in PB-00095490, the OSDI symptom score improved in both arms with no significant difference between them (see claims OG-27).
- **Treatment area.** In US copy the skin on the malar region, eyes fully covered; the US manual says "IPL treatment should not be applied to the eyelids." Do not describe light applied to the eyes or eyelids in US copy. The EU manual describes an eyelid step; only use it for markets where it is registered and reviewed.
- **Doxycycline patients.** Doxycycline is photosensitizing and must be stopped 3 months before treatment. Do not present current doxycycline users as ready-to-treat candidates without that caveat.
- **Repeat or maintenance treatment.** The US manual says safety and effectiveness of repeated use "was not evaluated". Maintenance schedules in brochures (6-12 months) need regulatory review before they are stated as supported.
- **Pregnancy.** Do not say or imply treatment is safe in pregnancy or nursing; the manual says there is "no clinical information", and the quick reference guide lists it as an absolute contraindication.
- **Pain and downtime.** The manuals tell practitioners to warn of discomfort or pain and transient erythema/edema. Avoid "painless" or "no side effects".

## FDA status

Carried over from the 2026-09-25 version of this page; it was checked against public FDA sources on that date and was not re-checked in this pass. None of the Lumenis documents on file quotes the FDA order itself; the manuals state only "FDA Classification: Class II Medical Device" (UM-0005500DIGAES Rev. H and UM-0005500DIG Rev. F, chapter 4).

- **Authorization:** FDA granted Lumenis's De Novo request [DEN200028](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/DEN200028.pdf) on February 23, 2021, for the Lumenis Stellar M22 IPL platform. The indication is the US text above. The order classified it Class II under 21 CFR 886.5201, "Intense pulsed light device for managing dry eye," product code QIU. Lumenis announced OptiLIGHT on this authorization on [April 29, 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lumenis-receives-fda-approval-for-its-ipl-device-to-manage-dry-eye-disease-and-launches-optilight-301280500.html).
- **"First and only":** the openFDA premarket database listed one device under product code QIU, the Lumenis De Novo, and no 510(k) clearances for other devices on the check date. Re-check it before each redistribution, because any competitor can now be cleared under this code.
- **Wording:** Lumenis's press release, [product page](https://lumenis.com/vision/products/optilight/) and trade coverage all describe OptiLIGHT as "FDA-approved." The PB-numbered pieces on file use the same wording: PB-00032060 Rev. C ("THE FIRST AND ONLY LIGHT THERAPY, FDA-APPROVED FOR DRY EYE MANAGEMENT"), PB-00029930 Rev. C ("the first and only light Therapy, FDA-approved for management of dry eye disease due to meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD)"), PB-00095930 ("The only IPL FDA approved for management of dry eye disease") and PB-00089280 Rev. A. Use the established Lumenis wording, and keep the qualifier:

> OptiLIGHT is the first and only IPL FDA-approved for the management of dry eye disease due to MGD.

The legal-precise term is a De Novo authorization. OptiLIGHT's "FDA-approved" wording does not transfer to OptiLIFT or OptiPLUS. Both are "FDA-cleared," and "FDA-approved" is a don't in their legal lists.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **EU wording conflict.** The newest EU manual (UM-0005500DIG Rev. F) states the EU indication as "Treatment of evaporative dry eye disease ... due to Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD)" without a skin-type limit, and says in section A.5.1 that skin type V is for "subjects of Indian descent ... approved in EU only." The Quick Reference Guide (PB-00094760 Rev. A) and B2B brochure (PB-00029930 Rev. C) state "Fitzpatrick skin types I-V" in the indication. Which wording is current for EU copy, and which markets are covered?
2. **US vs EU verb.** The US indication is "Improvement of signs of"; the EU indication is "Treatment of". Confirm that marketing for non-US markets may use "treatment" and that registrations outside the EU match the EU manual.
3. **Eyelid treatment.** The US manual (Rev. H) says "IPL treatment should not be applied to the eyelids" and "NOT intended for treatment over the eye or eyelids." The EU manual (Rev. F) describes OPT treatment "over the eyelids" with corneal shields, and the OPT Shields flyer (PB-00069990 Rev. B) says the shields block IPL energy "on the eyelids." The clinical studies book (PB-00095490 Rev. A p.2) says IPL on upper and lower eyelids "is safe and effective" and footnotes "Per regional regulatory approval". Confirm where eyelid treatment may be mentioned and that it never reaches US audiences.
4. **"FDA-approved" and "first and only."** Every PB-numbered piece on file uses "FDA-approved", but the manuals say only "Class II Medical Device". Confirm the preferred legal term (De Novo authorization) and who re-checks "first and only" before each reuse.
5. **Quick Reference Guide vs manual.** The guide lists pregnancy/nursing as absolute, isotretinoin as "6-12 months", 2-4 week intervals and 5-15 minute test patches; the manual says Lumenis has "no clinical information" on pregnancy, lists isotretinoin in the 3-month photosensitizing group, evaluated sessions "each two weeks apart", and recommends waiting "up to 15 minutes" (skin types I-III) or "up to 24-48 hours" (IV).
