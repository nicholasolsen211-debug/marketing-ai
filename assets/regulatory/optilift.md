# OptiLIFT regulatory language and do's and don'ts

OptiLIFT is an Eye Care product. The labeling differs by market: the US, EU and Canada user manuals do not carry the same indication text. Each market's wording is quoted separately below. Do not carry one market's wording into copy for another market.

## Indication for use (verbatim, by market)

### United States: User Manual 11600880 ver. 4, Section 3 "Indications", p.15

> The OptiLIFT System and its accessories are intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using VoluDerm Energy (Microneedling Applicator). It is also intended for use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for non-invasive treatment when using RF Energy and for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles (OptiLIFT Applicator). OptiLIFT System is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind. OptiLIFT System is intended to be operated by a licensed healthcare practitioner who is present to monitor treatment.

The same section 1 text (p.7) adds:

> Intended settings for the system: Clinics & Hospitals. Intended users: licensed healthcare practitioner.
>
> The OptiLIFT is a prescribed system intended for licensed healthcare practitioner users.

### European Union and Switzerland: User Manual 11600880EU Rev 02, Section 3 "Indications", p.16

> The OptiLIFT System and its accessories are intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using VoluDerm Energy (Microneedling Applicator). It is also intended for use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for non-invasive treatment when using RF Energy and for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles (OptiLIFT Applicator).. OptiLIFT System is intended to be operated by a licensed healthcare practitioner who is present to monitor treatment.

The EU text does not contain the US sentence "OptiLIFT System is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind." (The double full stop is in the source.)

### Canada: User Manual 11600880CA Rev 01, Section 3 "Indications", p.15

> The OptiLIFT system is indicated for use for skin ablation, resurfacing, skin tightening and wrinkles reduction, using the Microneedling applicator. The OptiLIFT applicator is intended for non-invasive skin tightening, wrinkles treatments, muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles and to reduce signs and symptoms of evaporative dry eye disease (DED), as well as improvement of lid laxity and blinking mechanism.

This is materially different from the US and EU wording. It is the only source on file that states a dry eye indication, and it is a Canada-only manual. See Open questions 1 and 2. Nothing on file says whether the Canadian text reflects a Health Canada licence.

## Contraindications (verbatim)

Sections 4.1 to 4.3 are word-for-word identical in the US (ver. 4), EU (Rev 02) and Canada (Rev 01) manuals (compared on file text). Quoted from the US manual, Section 4, p.16.

> **4.1. General contraindications**
> Patients who have the following conditions should not be treated:
> Powered muscle stimulators should not be used on patients with cardiac demand pacemakers
> Pacemaker, defibrillator, or any implanted electronic system
> Metal implants in the treatment area
> Pregnancy or nursing
> Severe concurrent illnesses or condition such as cancer, lupus, uncontrolled diabetes, uncontrolled seizure disorders
> Concurrent or chronic skin disorders or lesions in the treatment area
> Severe bleeding or vascular disorders
> Under 18 years of age
> Patients who cannot feel heat to give proper treatment feedback (nerve damage, etc.)
> Those who form keloids or heal poorly
> Injected chemical substances, threads, and synthetic fillers in the treated area.
> Fillers, collagen, fat injections or other injected biomaterial in the treated area within three months prior to treatment and not before complete healing has occurred, Botox in the treated area within 2 weeks prior to treatment.
>
> **4.2. OptiLIFT RF and Microneedling disposable tips related contraindications**
> Impaired immune system due to autoimmune diseases, immunosuppressive diseases or use of immunosuppressive medications
> Do not treat over tattoos or permanent make-up
> Those who have been on Accutane (Isotretinoin) within the last six months
>
> **4.3. Relative contraindications (treatable at the discretion):**
> History of Herpes or similar conditions that can be triggered by heat should be treated prophylactically with the appropriate medication
> Patients taking medications, herbal preparations, food supplements or vitamins that might cause fragile skin or impaired skin healing such as prolonged steroid regime, tetracyclines, or St. John's Wort.
> Excessively tanned skin from sun, tanning beds, or spray tans etc.
> Use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDS, e.g., ibuprofen containing agents). Best practice would allow one week before and after treatment without use
> Be sure that any other procedures such as surgery, laser treatments, chemical peels, injections, implants etc., are completely healed before treatment. Botox should be allowed to calm down at least 2-3 weeks before treatment, fillers 6 weeks plus, and more invasive procedures longer

## Warnings and risks

### Short form printed in marketing pieces (verbatim)

Source: PB-00094762 Rev. A p.8, PB-00088980 Rev B p.1, PB-00086630 Rev A p.6 and PB-00087460 Rev. A slide 75 (slide 75 uses different line breaks and capitalization but the same content).

> OptiLIFT System is contraindicated for patients with pacemakers, defibrillators, any implanted electronic device, or metal implanted in the treatment area. Side effects may appear either at the time of treatment or shortly after. Side effects may include any of the following: prolonged or significant pain, damage to natural skin texture (blister, burn), excessive skin redness (erythema), excessive swelling (edema), fragile skin bruising, excessive itching, change of pigmentation (hyper-pigmentation or hypopigmentation), scarring transient skin break-out such as acne and pimples. For a complete list of contraindications, please refer to the User Manual.

> **5.1. > **5.2. OptiLIFT RF energy, Microneedling disposable tips related side effects:** Damage to natural skin texture (blister, burn) · Change of pigmentation (hyper-pigmentation or hypopigmentation) · Scarring · Transient skin break-out such as acne and pimples

## What this means for copy

Read the market first. Points 1 to 3 rest on the US and EU labeling and on the legal word list; the Canada manual says something different (Open question 1).

- **OptiLIFT is not a dry eye treatment in US and EU copy.** The US indication is muscle conditioning of healthy muscles and states the device "is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind." The EU indication also names no medical condition. Never say or imply that OptiLIFT treats, improves or relieves dry eye, MGD or their signs and symptoms in US or EU copy. Only the Canada manual (Rev 01) states a dry eye indication, and that wording must not be reused outside Canada.
- **Describe what it does:** dynamic muscle stimulation (DMSt) and RF technologies that stimulate the muscles in the periorbital area, tone and tighten the lower eyelid, enhance blinking, and address lower lid laxity and impaired blinking. (Wording from the legal list; carried over.)
- **Study results only in study context.** "Improves TBUT" and "improves lower lid laxity and related blinking mechanics" are permitted only when speaking about the study, with the reference. Carry the study's own limits: PB-00087480 Rev. A states "The study was not powered. | The study did not include a control group. | Some exploratory outcomes were subjectively assessed by the investigator."
- **Location:** "applied on the skin in the peri-orbital area." The manual says "never treat over the eye lids & eyeballs." Do not mention proximity to the eye or treatment on the eyelids.
- **Regulatory status:** "FDA-cleared technology," never "FDA-approved." The only on-file statement of clearance is PB-00087480 Rev. A: "FDA-cleared for muscle conditioning and stimulating healthy muscles." Do not stretch "FDA-cleared" to dry eye, lid laxity or blinking.
- **Operator:** the current manuals (US, EU, Canada) and patient brochure Rev B say "licensed healthcare practitioner." Older short-form pieces say "trained professional" (Open question 3).
- **Dry eye statistics are about the condition, not the device.** Epidemiology from the [claims library](../claims-reference/optilift.md), such as lid-closure prevalence in dry eye patients, can describe patients. It must not be joined to OptiLIFT as a claimed outcome.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **Canada indication.** The Canada manual (11600880CA Rev 01) indicates the OptiLIFT applicator "to reduce signs and symptoms of evaporative dry eye disease (DED), as well as improvement of lid laxity and blinking mechanism." The US manual says the system "is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases," and the EU manual names no medical condition. Confirm what is actually authorized in each market and whether dry eye copy is allowed in Canada at all.
2. **EU manual revision.** The English EU manual file is named "rev.03" but its title page and footer read "11600880EU Rev 02." The French, German and Italian versions are Rev 03. Confirm the current EU revision and whether the English text on file is the newest. The EU manual's symbol table says "CE Mark according to the MDD"; confirm whether an MDR statement is current.
3. **Operator wording.** The US, EU and Canada manuals, patient brochure Rev B and the In-Service deck say "licensed healthcare practitioner." Quick Reference Guide PB-00094762 Rev. A and practice brochure PB-00086630 Rev A still say "trained professional." Confirm which is current and whether those two pieces need a new revision. Note also that the "Federal (US) law" caution says "physician."
4. **Treatment zone.** The manual says "never treat over the eye lids & eyeballs." In-Service slide 37 (PB-00087460 Rev. A) says "Treatment area: Inferior orbital rim, below the lower eyelids" and "Do not treat directly on the eyelids," but slide 48 lists "Prohibited treatment zones: periorbital region" under the OptiLIFT Applicator. Confirm the correct statement; the periorbital protocol and the slide 48 prohibition cannot both stand.
5. **FDA clearance wording.** Only PB-00087480 Rev. A states clearance, and no clearance number, letter or "Rx only" text is on file. Confirm the approved clearance wording and where it should travel.
6. **"Treatment."** The don't list rules out "Treatment," yet the indication, warnings and quick reference guide use "treatment," "treatment area" and "sessions." The likely reading is that OptiLIFT must not be called a treatment *for a condition*, while procedural words are acceptable. Confirm.
7. **Eyelid wording.** "Tone and tighten the lower eyelid" is a do, but "treatment on eyelids" is a don't. Confirm that "lower eyelid" may name the outcome as long as the copy says the device is applied to the skin in the peri-orbital area.
8. **Contraindications.** The Quick Reference Guide FAQ names insulin pumps and cochlear implants; the manual's contraindication list says "any implanted electronic system" and its warnings give a 15 cm distance for cochlear implants and pacemakers. Confirm the wording patient materials should use.
9. **"Clinically validated" footnote.** The legal PDF and PB-00086630 Rev A still cite "data on file; manuscript under preparation." Confirm the published Chelnis 2025 reference can replace it. In patient brochure Rev B the "70%" statement carries superscript 3, which now points to the Doan 2020 reference; the Chelnis reference is number 4.
10. **Dry eye positioning.** Existing sales materials pair OptiLIFT with dry eye patients (for example, the Chelnis study's dry eye population and lid-closure epidemiology). Confirm how far dry eye context may appear in OptiLIFT pieces given the US "not intended ... for medical diseases or medical conditions" sentence.
11. **Legal word list.** The legal do's and don'ts document was not re-read for this update and is undated. Confirm it is still current, and whether a separate list is needed for the OptiLIFT Face (aesthetics) modality, which the manuals describe as an optional license.
12. **Consumer poster.** PB-00094710 Rev A ("Lift and tighten sagging lower lids ... no downtime") carries no indication or warnings text. Confirm whether that channel requires it.
