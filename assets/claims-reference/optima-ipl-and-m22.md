# Optima IPL and M22 claims reference (legacy platform)

> **LEGACY PLATFORM.** Optima IPL / M22 (eye care) and Stellar M22 (aesthetics) is a legacy platform. Optima IPL is the predecessor of OptiLIGHT, and **OptiLIGHT is the current dry eye product**. **Nothing in this file may be used for OptiLIGHT claims**, and OptiLIGHT claims may not be used here. The same statistic can appear in both files with a different source document and a different qualification; do not move a row, a figure or a citation from one product to the other. Check [regulatory language](../regulatory/optima-ipl-and-m22.md) first: the dry eye indication for this platform has not been cleared or authorized by the FDA in the United States.

Source materials reviewed for this pass (control IDs and revisions confirmed from each document's own footer):

- **Brochure 2021** - "Stellar M22 brochure" (body text: Optima IPL), PB-00024560 Rev A, 6-page web file, copyright 2021. Newest eye-care piece on file.
- **Brochure 2019** - Optima IPL brochure, PB-2012273 Rev A, 2-page print file (6-page web file has the same text), copyright 2019.
- **Brochure 2018** - Optima IPL "Think Outside the Eye", PB-2005802 Rev C, 2-page print file (6-page web file has the same text), copyright 2018.
- **Patient trifold 2016** - Optima IPL Patient Trifold, PB-2006203 Rev A, 2 pages, copyright 2016.

## Notes and flags

- **Scope and exclusions.** Claims without a printed reference were not added. Examples: "Dry Eye Disease (DED) is the most common condition seen by eye care professionals" (PB-2005802 Rev C), "~20 different skin indications" (PB-2012273 Rev A, PB-00024560 Rev A, PB-2005802 Rev C), the 80%-of-rosacea and 20%-ocular-rosacea statements in the addendum UM-1034720 Rev A section A.5.1 (not a PB-numbered marketing document and no citation), and aesthetic trifold claims such as "Stimulating new collagen production is the key to a smoother, tighter looking skin" (PB-2020010 Rev B, PB-2020026 Rev B, PB-2020050 Rev B). Physician and patient testimonials (Toyos, Periman, Gaster, patient quotes) are not claims and are not included. Before-and-after photographs ("Courtesy of ...") carry no reference and are not claims here.
- **Ge et al. 2020** (BMC Ophthalmology 20:111, doi 10.1186/s12886-020-01357-5) is on file as a published study of M22 OPT in cataract and MGD patients in China. No PB-numbered Lumenis piece on file cites it, so it cannot be an "Appears in" source and is not a row. If a Lumenis piece cites it later, add a row.
- **Currency across the eye-care pieces.** The 2018, 2019 and 2021 eye-care brochures have different PB numbers rather than revisions of one number, so none is formally superseded. In date order, the dry eye "vicious cycle" content (OI-2, OI-4, OI-5, OI-6, OI-8, OI-11, OI-12) appears only in the 2016 and 2018 pieces, and the 2019 and 2021 pieces drop it and reposition around periocular inflammation and rosacea. Rows whose only source is 2016 or 2018 are marked "Not in the 2019 or 2021 brochures" in the qualifications. Treat them as historical, not current.
- **OI-10 is the clearest citation inconsistency.** Demodex is cited to Prieto 2002 in two pieces and to Papageorgiou 2008 in the other two. Confirm against the papers before any reuse.
- **No DOI or PMID is printed in any of these pieces.** Every reference is a journal citation in the printed form (authors, year, journal, volume(issue):pages), usually without a title. None was located or opened in this pass. All rows therefore stay `needs review`, and no row is `confirmed`.
- **Market limit.** Every dry eye outcome row (OI-6 to OI-8, OI-11, OI-12) is subject to the regulatory statement that the dry eye indication has not been cleared or authorized in the US. Do not use these rows in US copy.
- **Not OptiLIGHT.** The OptiLIGHT claims file contains figures that look similar (for example a tear break-up time improvement). Those are cited to different documents and are OptiLIGHT claims. They are not evidence for this legacy platform, and the rows above are not evidence for OptiLIGHT.
