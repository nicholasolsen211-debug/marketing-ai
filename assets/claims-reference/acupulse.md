# AcuPulse claims reference

Check [regulatory language](../regulatory/acupulse.md) first. A reference existing for a statement does not mean the statement is an approved claim for the product. In particular, the FemTouch / vaginal atrophy rows (AP-17, AP-18) describe an application that Lumenis's own 2025 brochure and pitch deck say is not cleared by the FDA in the US.

Source materials reviewed for this pass (control IDs and revisions confirmed from each document's own footer or title text):

- **Case studies, Indian skin** - "Use of CO2 laser for the treatment of acne and burn scars in Indian skin - case studies" (Dr. Aditya Shah), PB-2010525 Rev A, 8 pages, no copyright year printed. Four files on file (A4 and Letter, press and web) carry the same revision and text. Source of AP-1 to AP-11.
- **Gold white paper** - "AcuPulse with Combo Treatment Modality: Clinical Study of Patients with Skin of Color" (Michael H. Gold, MD), January 2012, copyright 2012. The index and the front matter give PB-1106140 Rev A; the page footer prints "PB-1116140 rev A". Source of AP-12 and AP-13.
- **Skinbooster white paper** - "Clinical experience with SkinBoosters and ablative and non-ablative laser combination treatments" (De Angelis, Tretti Clementoni), PB-1007586 Rev B, 4 pages, a print date of 08.06.16 on the first page, no copyright year printed. Source of AP-14 to AP-16.
- **AcuPulse W Clinical Treatment Guide, USA only** - PB-00021600 Rev B, 8 pages, copyright 2020. Source of AP-17. (A different document, the AcuPulse W brochure for the USA, is also numbered PB-00021600, Rev A; see the regulatory page.)
- **AcuPulse and FemTouch Pitch Deck** - printed "PB-0009551_A" (seven digits, no year printed), 50 slides. Source of AP-18.

No claims file for this product existed on main or in the unmerged branch, so IDs start at AP-1. Existing rows kept: 0. Marked superseded: 0. New rows: 18.

## Notes and flags

- **Scope and exclusions.** Claims without a printed reference were not added. The pitch deck's "CLINICALLY VALIDATED" slide (slide 18) quotes "Wen-Chu, et al. 2025", "Munavalli, 2013" and "De Angelis, 2012" with only an author and year, no journal, title, DOI or PMID, so they are not traceable and are not rows. The deck's women's health market slide cites "Urinary Incontinence in US Women, A Population Based Study. Arch Intern Med. 2005" (no authors or pages) and a 2013 NAMS position statement (no journal citation); the percentages those footnotes support cannot be read from the extracted text, so no rows were written.
- **The Indian-skin paper's reference list is run together in the extracted text.** The numbers 1-18 appear as a single line, then the entries in order, and 19-26 likewise. The mapping used above counts the entries in order. It is consistent with the superscripts for references 6, 11, 21 to 26 but cannot be confirmed from the text alone. Confirm against the PDF.
- **Statements in PB-2010525 left out because they do not map cleanly to a reference.** "In a recent review of the literature, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) was observed in up to 92% of ablative fractional laser-treated patients" (p.4) has no reference number attached. "A retrospective study of 82 Saudi Arabian patients ... at least 50% improvement in scar severity in 37% of patients" (p.4) carries superscript 21, but reference 21 (Alexis, a review) does not appear to be that study; the description matches reference 26 (Alajlan) more closely, and the 14% Middle Eastern figure (AP-10) is also attributed to 26. The silicone gel guideline sentence (p.3, superscript 8) is attributed to reference 8, a 2017 burn scar laser paper, which does not read as a guideline. Treatments for atrophic acne scars (p.3, references 5 and 7) is a list, not a statistic. Review these in the PDF before adding rows.
- **PB-2010525 results are not rows.** Statements such as "Both patients achieved improvement", "very high" satisfaction and "mild and transient" adverse events describe two unpublished case reports and cannot be cited as study results. The conclusion ("fractional ablative CO2 laser treatment of acne and burn scars is effective and causes mild and transient adverse effects") has no reference attached.
- **PB-1106140 Gold study.** The 15-subject Combo study (investigator-rated "good-very good" in 50%, wrinkle score 4.3 to 2.8, at least 65% rated results good-very good, more than 85% satisfied) cites Gold and Biron, "J Cos Las Ther. Submitted for publication." That is not a published reference, so it was not added. The statement that non-ablative fractional lasers need 4-6 treatments over 4-6 months cites "Lumenis 2008" (reference 3, no journal), also not added. The catalog number is inconsistent within the document (see the regulatory page, open question 13).
- **PB-00007620 Rev A (CO2 Clinical Publications Review, Vol. 1 Issue 1) was reviewed and not used.** It carries four full journal citations with DOIs, but the studies concern UltraPulse (DeepFX, SCAAR FX) and, in one case, M22 IPL, not AcuPulse, and the review's own text says "UltraPulse is Above and Beyond". Under the rule against borrowing claims from another product, none was added. If AcuPulse and UltraPulse are confirmed as covered by one shared claim set, rows can be added then.
- **Documents reviewed with no referenced claim found:** the 2025 B2B brochure PB-00082580 Rev A, the AcuPulse Resurfacing B2C brochure PB-00077240 Rev A (2024), the AcuPulse W one-pager PB-00021590 Rev A, the AcuPulse W brochure PB-00021600 Rev A, and the 2018 brochure PB-1135190 Rev H. None prints a DOI, PMID or journal citation for a statistic. Their statements ("minimal downtime", "comfortable", "safe") are marketing wording; see the regulatory page, open question 12.
- **Older material not used for current claims:** the CO2 Compendium PB-1001651 Rev D (2017, old "Energy to Healthcare" branding and UltraPulse content), the acne scar patient pieces PB-1005691 and related (Rev C, 2017, which describe DeepFX and ActiveFX of the UltraPulse and AcuPulse systems), the competitor comparison sheets (2013-2014) and the 2012 EADV press release (not a controlled document). The Goldman and Butterwick 2011 item, the Miyata mole removal paper and the "AcuPulse" clinical file in the clinical evidence folder have no PB number and cannot be named in "Appears in".
- **Never borrow claims from other products.** Statistics for UltraPulse, M22, Stellar M22, ResurFX or Restylane that appear in these documents are not AcuPulse claims. In particular, the skinbooster-plus-ResurFX neck study cited in PB-1007586 Rev B (reference 12, nine subjects) is a non-ablative laser study and was left out.
- **Verification.** Every "Appears in" entry and every printed reference above was read in the extracted text of the document named. No cited paper was opened or checked against its source in this pass, and no DOI was resolved; all rows therefore stay `needs review`.
